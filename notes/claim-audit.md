# Claim audit — do not silently inherit the conversation’s overstatements

**Status:** initial audit, 2026-10-10; expanded after Aaron’s revision `6801a0b` for dogmatism and nondual reading. Earlier assistant answers supplied useful leads but were not uniformly reliable. This file governs promotion from archive to active prose.

| Claim or formulation | Assessment and required handling |
|---|---|
| “All will be forgiven” proves every person’s final salvation. | Too strong. In Mark 3:28 the scope is specified as human sins and blasphemies. Keep verse 29’s qualification in view. [B-02](../sources/biblical-texts.md#b-02). |
| Mark literally says “not forgiven until the end of the age.” | Incorrect. No “until” or “end” appears. The negative idiom normally means never. Matthew 28:20 has the distinct end-of-age expression. [B-03](../sources/biblical-texts.md#b-03), [L-01](../sources/greek-and-semantic-history.md#l-01). |
| “Age-enduring” is the closest neutral English translation. | It can be a study gloss, but risks implying finitude not established in context. Default to “eternal” in ordinary English; discuss alternatives separately. |
| “Does not have forgiveness” refers only to the present. | Incorrect inference: the temporal qualification must be read with the present tense. Grammar alone does not establish a conditional “while.” |
| The aorist of “blaspheme” means repeated, persistent action. | Incorrect. Neither habitual repetition nor a single irreparable slip follows from the aorist alone. Mark 3:30’s imperfect supplies narrative background, not an explicit condition for pardon. [L-05](../sources/greek-and-semantic-history.md#l-05). |
| Aphesis means psychological release rather than divine pardon. | Overreach. Release is part of the lexical range; forgiveness/remission is appropriate in the sin context. Do not import all senses into one occurrence. [L-03](../sources/greek-and-semantic-history.md#l-03). |
| “Offense” is a distinct word in the passage. | No. It is an optional English gloss for hamartēma, not additional Greek vocabulary. Avoid it when it inadvertently suggests a modern criminal code. [L-02](../sources/greek-and-semantic-history.md#l-02). |
| There are no legal associations until later theology. | False. Enochos already denotes liability in ancient usage, including Mark 14:64. Absence of an explicit punishment noun does not exclude a judicial interpretation. [L-04](../sources/greek-and-semantic-history.md#l-04). |
| “Sin” means merely missing the mark or wrong perception. | Insufficient. Etymology is not a complete meaning. Wrong perception is a proposed spiritual analysis, not a replacement for accountability or the full biblical semantic field. |
| Meaning progresses linearly from relational innocence to punitive legalism. | Not demonstrated. Legal, relational, cultic, therapeutic, and economic associations overlap in antiquity and later reception. Study dated examples and counterexamples. [S-02–S-03](../sources/modern-scholarship.md#s-02). |
| The “judgment” manuscript reading proves deliberate doctrinal corruption. | Not established. The variant exists; scribal motive and exact transmission history require an apparatus and witnesses. It illustrates interpretation, not a verified one-way conspiracy. |
| Absence of chronos establishes nonlinear time. | No. Absence of a word does not establish a philosophy. Aiōn/aiōnios cannot bear that argument alone. Plum Village interbeing of time is a declared comparison. |
| Matthew’s “neither age” allows forgiveness at the end of this age. | Opposed by Matthew’s explicit inclusion of the coming age. Beyond-both-ages readings are constructive theology, not a straightforward translation. |
| The scribes knowingly lied about what they privately recognized as divine. | A possible theological interpretation, not Mark’s explicit report of their minds. Do not substitute the narrator’s evaluation for historical mind-reading. |
| Paul committed the precise sin in Mark 3:29. | Unproven. He is absent from that story; no cited source attributes that accusation to him. He is a canonical test case for changed religious judgment. |
| Galatians, Acts, and 1 Timothy are one autobiographical testimony. | Incorrect. Separate Paul’s letters, Acts’ narrative, and the Pastorals’ portrait; 1 Timothy’s authorship is disputed. [B-05–B-08](../sources/biblical-texts.md#b-05). |
| Paul’s transformation removed every hard boundary in his teaching. | No. Galatians 1:8–9 itself uses severe exclusionary language. Humanity must include this evidence too. |
| John’s sequence is “remain → become a disciple → know.” | Correct the middle: “you are truly my disciples” is present. John 8:31 also addresses those described as having believed. Do not oppose all belief to practice. |
| John 7:17 says doing alone guarantees knowledge. | It refers to willingness to do God’s will and discernment of Jesus’s teaching. John 3:21’s “doing truth” has ethical-covenantal context, not merely experimental verification. |
| Jesus likely regretted specific actions. | Speculative; no basis supplied in this project. Remove as historical assertion. |
| Full humanity necessarily entails doctrinal or moral error. | A contested theological inference, not a historical result. Traditional Christology affirms real humanity and sinlessness. State the project’s stronger proposals as proposals. |
| Chrysostom simply teaches that every case is reversible. | Incomplete. Homily 41 includes repentance language but also punishment here and hereafter and anti-Jewish polemic. Preserve that context. [R-02](../sources/interpretation-history.md#r-02). |
| Aquinas guarantees universal postmortem restoration. | No. His allowance for extraordinary divine healing is within a discussion also affirming final impenitence. [R-04](../sources/interpretation-history.md#r-04). |
| MacDonald’s “whole age or era” comes from *It Shall Not Be Forgiven*. | Locator correction: the combined Gutenberg volume places this passage in *Light*; the “fixed, a final condition” statement is in *It Shall Not Be Forgiven*. [R-06](../sources/interpretation-history.md#r-06). |
| “The three times inter-are” is verified here as an exact TNH quotation. | The direct verified passage is Sister Annabel Laity’s *The Three Dharma Seals*. She presents it as a Plum Village teaching. Keep attribution exact. [PV-04](../sources/plum-village/time-and-transformation.md#pv-04). |
| A Plum Village talk description is a transcript quotation. | No. Editorial summaries and transcript excerpts must be identified separately. Record review status and verify audio before attributing exact words. |
| “Grace makes consequences valuable” justifies past harm. | No. Learning from consequences does not make suffering necessary, desirable, deserved, or excusable. |
| Rigidity or dogmatism is the lexical meaning of blasphemy. | No. These are working interpretive categories. Mark’s hardening language provides a comparison, not an equivalence. Do not identify every disagreement, uncertainty, or protective boundary as culpable refusal. |
| The First Training rejects all doctrines or moral commitments. | It rejects attachment and dogmatism, including attachment to Buddhist teachings; the trainings themselves are commitments. Read the Second Training alongside it. [ND-01](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-01--openness-and-nonattachment-to-views). |
| Modern logic requires permanent personal identities or prevents thinking about change. | Not established and not needed. Distinguish valid inference from true premises and adequate descriptions. A present condition does not imply its permanence without an added premise. [ND-08](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-08--logic-and-the-choice-of-a-description). |
| Buddhist logic is one system that permits any contradiction. | Misleading. No such general account is established here. The essay uses TNH’s critique of fixed notions, not a surveyed rival calculus. “Not one, not two” need not be treated as a numerical contradiction. |
| The two truths mean that Mark’s two clauses are both true in the same respect despite contradiction. | Not the proposal. Mark’s general statement plus exception need not be formally inconsistent. Do not simply assign one clause to conventional truth and the other to ultimate truth. These Buddhist teachings concern a way of seeing, not an automatic solution to syntax. |
| Historical/ultimate dimensions and conventional/ultimate truth are identical terms in every Buddhist school. | No. Attribute the relationship to the specific TNH interview and Plum Village teaching. Do not make ordinary accountability a disposable “lower” truth. [ND-02–ND-03](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-02--historical-and-ultimate-neither-monism-nor-separation). |
| Nondual insight erases responsibility or requires reconciliation. | No. Our application holds continuity and transformation together. A person remains answerable for past action without being reduced to it. Difference, consent, and boundaries remain ethically significant. |
| The Samaritan is a generic member of a wholly unrelated religion. | In John 4, the woman identifies Jacob as an ancestor; the dispute involves an intertwined Israelite inheritance. Specify the boundary rather than flattening it. Luke’s mercy teaching begins with Jewish scriptural commandments. [ND-06](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-06--the-neighbor-and-worship-beyond-rival-locations). |
| Mark 9 proves all religions or all claimed healings are equally true. | No. The exorcist works in Jesus’s name, though outside the disciples’ group. The episode challenges group control; it does not eliminate discernment. [ND-05](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-05--a-healing-outside-the-disciples-group). |
| John 17 establishes Buddhist metaphysics or reports the historical Jesus’s interior experience directly. | No. Mutual indwelling invites comparison; Johannine communion has its own context and particular commitments. [ND-07](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-07--unity-without-erasing-relation). |
| Mark 3:29 is historically a Zen koan. | No. A koan-like approach is a proposed practice of reading in which the reader is implicated. It does not replace historical or ethical inquiry. TNH’s imagined roles in *Bat Nha* are not separate witness transcripts. [ND-04](../sources/nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-04--a-koan-involving-the-reader). |

## Substantive unresolved questions

1. Does the warning concern an act, a disposition, a relation, or a rhetorically urgent conjunction of these?
2. What independent historical basis supports any reconstruction before Mark?
3. How far can a conditional-resistance interpretation go without neutralizing the negative temporal idiom?
4. What does final impenitence add to the text, and why is death decisive in that theology?
5. How do early Jewish sources hold pardon together with consequences? Avoid contrasting Jewish severity with Christian mercy.
6. What survives of the proposed interpretation under the strongest irreversible-hardening reading?
7. How can practice inform interpretation without making preference or one community’s experience self-validating?
8. What can nondual reading disclose while preserving the distinction between a source’s claims and our constructive use of it?
9. What would count against the essay’s account of rigidity, and how would we recognize that evidence?
