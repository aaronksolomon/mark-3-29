## Scope and method

Make the online evidence accessible through the bibliography and selected footnotes. Prefer the exact consulted edition, translator, chapter, or institutional source. Retain author, work, edition, and stable locator so the reference also works in print. Use descriptive linked source names rather than bare URLs or repeated “click here.” Link a footnote where access to the cited text, a difficult claim, translation choice, or important qualification helps the reader; routine verse citations need not all become links.

A destination check establishes that the page opens and matches the named source and relevant passage. It is not a new comprehensive source verification, permissions clearance, independent specialist check, or completion of a pending book review. Preserve distinctions between full text, excerpt, abstract, subscription access, and catalog record. Never substitute another translation merely because its page is easier to access. Prefer stable page URLs; add fragment anchors only after inspecting them. Do not treat a browser/tool failure as proof of a dead link.
