## Editorial changes

The introduction keeps the author’s opening and questions, adds one sentence of scene orientation, and follows the approved comparative sequence: a reason for inquiry across traditions, Gospel examples, Jewish inheritance and continuing voices, Islamic listening within an Abrahamic orientation, then Thích Nhất Hạnh and Buddhist practice. It does not rank traditions or claim historical influence among the compared teachings.

Qur’an 39:18 is presented as a complete verse in Saheeh International, with the context of verse 17 supplied. The passage’s meaning is not reduced to generic pluralism. Al-Ghazālī’s account of Sufi practice is introduced in section 4, followed by the full health/satiety comparison from the identified English translation and an application to his examination of his own teaching motives.

The Syriac section is rebuilt around complete Peshitta Mark 3:29 and 3:30. It explicitly distinguishes the attested Syriac, the simplified conventional romanization, and the English translation supplied for this essay. Critical words are identified in the English brackets. The discussion no longer mentions the judgment-ending before the reader has seen the verse that leads to it. The Peshitta’s judgment-language is retained rather than silently replaced with the critical Greek’s sin-language.

The duplicate promise-and-warning paragraph in section 2 is removed. The following reversal is repaired by naming the claim being challenged. The paragraph after the Diamond Sutra says that the expression in Mark 3 does not have the same apparently contradictory structure as that passage; it then returns to the understanding through which both passages are read. The revised Mark 4 exposition and its open questions remain unchanged.

Qur’an 16:125 now appears as a complete relevant unit after an introduction identifying the invitation to God’s way. Its English is supplied for this essay and explicitly distinguished from the named translations used for comparison. The previous short Khattab phrases are not relabeled as a complete quotation.

The conclusion now gathers what the inquiry has made more visible: the human encounter, the difference between familiarity and hearing, the continuation of accusations through conduct and influence, repentance and repair, and humanity without diminution of divinity. It retains “anemic in practice” and the final question including “perhaps ourselves.” Its force is not a claim to have established the accusers’ eventual fate.

## Verification scope

Targeted source checks in this pass cover the displayed Saheeh International Qur’an 39:18 and its verse-17 setting, Qur’an 2:136 for the prophetic inheritance, Arabic and named English comparisons for 16:125, Claud Field’s 1909 al-Ghazālī title page and relevant “Sufism” pages, both Peshitta verses in Dukhrana, and the Peshitta/temporal lexical orientation. The new dossier gives access points and limits.

These are displayed-text, edition, and contextual checks—not a full review of the Arabic commentary tradition, a Syriac manuscript collation, an Arabic critical edition of al-Ghazālī, or specialist certification of the supplied translations. The romanization is a reading aid, not reconstructed first-century pronunciation. No complete fresh audit of the inherited Greek, NIV quotations, or earlier patristic sources is claimed.
