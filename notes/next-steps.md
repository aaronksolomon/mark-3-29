## Immediate reading and polishing

1. **Read the transitions in context before revising again.** The new bridges connect repentance with the historical interpretations, attention to others with Jesus’s humanity, the only-child image with non-exhaustive description, and temporal continuation with compassionate speech. Confirm that they help the thought unfold rather than explain the essay’s procedure.
2. **Preserve the difference between return and repetition.** The central concern can reappear in Jesus’s warning, later interpretation, and our conduct. Do not delete a sentence merely because its vocabulary has appeared before. Make a local change when the referent is unclear or the sentence adds no new movement.
3. **Keep the textual questions together.** The sin/judgment paragraph now sits with the Syriac discussion. The new question before the Christian interpreters concerns people already making the accusation and what recognition could mean for them. Do not infer a response Mark does not narrate.
4. **Read section 5 for continuity.** Keep the human reason for the only-child discussion, the full humanity/divinity paragraph, and the invitation to listen without prescribing the result. Its temporal discussion concerns present responsibility; Augustine’s subtle temporal account coexists with final impenitence.
5. **Retain the preferred hearing and its real limits.** Matthew 12:32 remains the two-age challenge. Mark 4 concerns the teaching’s role in disclosure and concealment, not only insufficient attention. Neither precision requires turning the whole essay back into a logical debate about finality.
6. **Let the ending stand unless a concrete problem emerges.** The invitation is not a summary to compress further. Preserve “anemic in practice,” the fuller return to shared humanity, and the question about what might become possible if we listened again.

Keep the complete print-visible Greek and John’s abiding/doing-truth passages. The current pass leaves the Greek, romanizations, and literal translations intact; only the natural-English labels are standardized. Targeted checks concerned Mark 4:10–25, Pesachim 10:5’s Hebrew, the Peshitta ending, and Augustine’s relevant English passages. These are not a new manuscript collation or comprehensive specialist review.

## Jewish study: next bounded pass

Use [Jewish contexts and cross-tradition inquiry](../sources/jewish-context-and-cross-tradition-inquiry.md) as the starting record.

- **Sabbath:** examine first-century diversity and emergency/non-emergency treatment. Later Mishnah Yoma 8:6 cannot be retrojected as a complete account of the scene in Mark 3.
- **Repentance and repair:** compare Exodus 34, Ezekiel 33, Ben Sira 28, Mishnah Yoma 8:9, and Maimonides, Laws of Repentance 2 and 6. Record what kind of harm, pardon, duty, and consequence each discusses.
- **Contemporary Jewish interpretation:** obtain and read Rabbi Danya Ruttenberg’s On Repentance and Repair. Only the author’s description has been checked. Attend to differences as well as agreements with the draft’s framework.
- **Method:** retain the existing Barr and Anderson priorities. Build dated examples rather than deriving a theology from etymologies. A Jewish scholar or teacher should review the treatment of Torah, mercy, and hardening.

## Additions held in reserve

The fig tree/Temple sequence, the woman threatened with stoning, and blind-guide comparisons are recorded at JC-11. John 7:53–8:11 needs manuscript-history study; it cannot simply establish original Johannine composition or a general account of Jewish law. A symbolic fig-tree reading must not become an allegory of permanent Jewish rejection.

Linji’s teachings in Thích Nhất Hạnh’s Zen Battles and The Sun My Heart remain reading leads. Locate a brief primary passage and stable locator before replacing the verified Bat Nha comparison. A catalog description is not the book.

A separate compact chronology should distinguish Gospel composition, surviving manuscript dates and languages, and earlier source traditions. Mark’s Greek composition does not establish a transcript of Jesus; Thomas’s priority has not been demonstrated here. Do not expand the main essay with speculative manuscript history.

## Continuing controls

Compassion is the author’s guiding commitment, not a reason to describe all disagreement as cruelty or dogmatism. An act of warning may express concern while its threatened outcome remains disputed. Mark records no reply between the parabolic argument and warning, and verse 30 relates the warning to an accusation already being made. Refusal of healing is an interpretive possibility, not a grammatical proof that forgiveness automatically follows a change of mind.

The wider [research queue](../sources/to-find.md) remains: critical commentaries, the full Kręcidło article, manuscript apparatus, original-language patristic editions, and a fuller historical-semantic corpus. None is represented as completed in this pass.
