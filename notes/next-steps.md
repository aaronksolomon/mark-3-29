## Next writing priorities

1. **Test the narrative aloud.** Sections 1–2 should allow a reader to follow the story without knowing the prior research. Does the movement from accusation through parabolic response to warning feel human and urgent without inventing a missing reply? The promise may heighten the warning; do not require it to be merely rhetorical.
2. **Assess the new balance.** The Greek remains in an expandable study note in section 3. The evidence is available, but the reader can follow the inquiry without repeated grammatical interruptions. Decide whether the reception paragraphs still need more connective language or whether further detail belongs only in sources.
3. **Review the terminology in section 4.** Clinging to certainty emphasizes a way of holding a view. Dogmatism is its doctrinal expression, not a label for all doctrine. Avoid turning either term into a judgment about a reader who favors finality or a person maintaining a protective boundary.
4. **Read section 5 especially carefully.** Humanization should draw Jesus closer without assuming divinity must be removed. The only-child comparison is new and deliberately bounded. John’s unique Son and the sutta’s practice of boundless care are not the same assertion. Keep the image only insofar as it helps the essay attend to real persons.
5. **Keep the koan-like inquiry concrete.** What changes in the reader’s conduct, listening, or treatment of others? Do not use paradox to make evidence irrelevant or severe teaching immune to scrutiny. Retain the Third Training’s challenge to coercion.
6. **Revisit the close after the body.** One practical question is enough. Preserve accountability and the unsettled question of finality without restarting the whole logical argument.
