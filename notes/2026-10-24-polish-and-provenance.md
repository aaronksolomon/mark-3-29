**Primary translator’s sources consulted:** Ibrahim Gamard, [“Apparently Irreligious Verses in the Works of Mawlana Rumi,”](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/apparently-irreligious-verses.html) section A, discussion of unbelief; [“About the Quatrains Concordances.”](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/quatrains_concord.html)

The familiar English field poem is associated with Coleman Barks’s version, based on John Moyne’s translation, rather than established here as a Daniel Ladinsky translation. Gamard locates it in *Open Secret* (1984), p. 8, and *The Essential Rumi* (1995), p. 36. His concordance connects it with Foruzanfar quatrain 395 / Farhadi–Gamard 1314.

Gamard’s discussion distinguishes Barks’s rightdoing/wrongdoing language from the Persian religious terms concerning Islam and unbelief. That difference is directly relevant to this essay’s care with translation. The popular wording should not be made to prove that ethical discernment disappears in nondual experience. A fuller comparison would require a deliberate choice among the Persian text, a scholarly translation, and Barks’s creative version.

**Editorial choice:** retain the existing Moses-and-shepherd narrative, which contributes directly to the effects of correction within a life. Keep the field poem as an available lead, not another main-text digression. The complete Barks books were not read in this pass.
