## The central editorial change

The question in Mark 4 is no longer organized chiefly as a search for a logically closed explanation of exclusion. The quoted words remain severe and visibly connected to Isaiah. Their possible work within a compassionate address becomes a question: what might hearing this inherited saying bring someone to see? Disclosure, explanation, and receptivity are held in relation without claiming that the grammar establishes only one account of divine purpose.

The quotation from Mark 4:12 is given continuously after the setting and the phrase “so that.” The larger narrative supplies a lamp meant to be seen, disciples who still need explanation, and seed growing beyond the sower's understanding. These images support attention to awakening without proving that every hearer will awaken or that understanding is simply a voluntary achievement. “Parabolic teaching can both reveal and expose an inability to hear” remains a central sentence.

Deep looking now has a positive exposition in section 4. The Second Mindfulness Training supplies exact brief phrases; concrete questions connect study to assumed meaning, fear, loyalty, conduct, and effects. Reason and contemplation remain participants rather than rivals. The Diamond Sutra is heard through short excerpts before its implication is discussed.

## What lasts, and what changes?

The temporal section now begins with the positive question of persistence through changing conditions. Plum Village's neither-same-nor-different teaching clarifies how an act can continue without an immutable bearer or identical result. It does not establish that every karmic consequence lasts infinitely.

Passover, Augustine, John, and Laity remain. John 12's grain image is added within its context of Jesus's death and discipleship. Ezekiel 18 and Qur'an 25:70 offer distinct accounts of a changed relation to previous wrongdoing through divine mercy. They are not identified with karma or no-self. The question is how a real past may be carried differently, not how to pretend it never happened.

The Islamic contribution is deliberately bounded: Qur'an 16:125 on religious address in the main text, and Qur'an 25:70 alongside Ezekiel on transformed deeds. Qur'an 39:53–54 is retained in the source notes. These are later comparative voices with their own claims, not sources for reconstructing Jesus's speech.
