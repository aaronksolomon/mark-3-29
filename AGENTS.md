# Research and editing conventions

## Purpose and voice

Aaron directs this writing project. The current direction is scholarly, readable, open-ended inquiry into Mark 3:28–30, semantic history, and the humanity of teachers and interpreters. Do not imitate Thích Nhất Hạnh’s literary voice. Do not turn the inquiry into a predetermined defense of universalism, inerrancy, or biblical error. Personal narrative is optional and author-controlled.

### Editorial voice — established by Aaron’s revisions

The essay is a shared inquiry, not a verdict delivered from outside the tradition. Prefer “we,” “our histories,” and questions about how we receive and live a teaching. Let Jesus, the evangelists, their interlocutors, later teachers, and ourselves remain human participants. Preserve Aaron’s movement from a particular saying toward perception, relationship, and practice.

Use clear, connected paragraphs with an unhurried but direct rhythm. “Look deeply,” “consider,” “touch,” and “become open” belong to Aaron’s vocabulary when they name a real act of attention; do not scatter them as decorative spiritual language. Avoid imitative cadences, chains of isolated one-line sentences, grand declarations, and repeated “perhaps.” A question should identify something genuinely unresolved, not disguise a settled accusation. State established observations directly; save question marks for real questions.

**Aaron’s `8dd280b` preference:** avoid reader-facing commentary about the essay’s procedure: “this essay,” “the reader,” “we will explore,” “as we will see,” and repeated explanations that a proposal is being made. Let the exposition perform the inquiry. Inclusive “we” still belongs when it concerns shared human experience, rather than giving a roadmap. Connect each paragraph to the preceding question instead of repeatedly announcing a new topic.

Distinguish strong conviction from dogmatism. Name harm and responsibility plainly without making a person or tradition the embodiment of blindness. Do not flatten anger, severity, compassion, and fallibility into mutually exclusive categories. Prefer specific descriptions—scribes, those questioning Jesus, those making the accusation—to a standing label of opponents. Keep *grammateis* at its first main-text occurrence, with a brief explanation of scribes as learned interpreters of Torah; Aaron’s latest revision places the Greek script γραμματεῖς and the NIV's “teachers of the law” comparison in the note. This does not require hiding explicit conflict or assigning benevolent motives the text does not record. “Encounter,” “exchange,” “disagreement,” or “debate” usually reads more naturally than the scholarly genre term “controversy”; choose the word for the particular event rather than banning “dispute” everywhere.

The current v4 emphasis is a compassionate wake-up call within a human encounter. Develop this as a qualified preferred hearing, not only an endlessly deferred question. Distinguish what the saying asserts, what speaking it may seek to accomplish, and what ultimately happens to its hearers. Compassionate purpose does not prove reversibility or excuse intimidation. Claims about Jesus’s historical intention remain proportionate to their evidence. A principled contrary reading must not be diagnosed as dogmatism.

Polish locally before rewriting whole sections, unless Aaron explicitly requests a major revision. Active v4 is a substantive publication revision developed from v3; the immediately preceding v3 remains frozen for comparison. Keep author edits unless accuracy, grammar, or coherence requires a change; make material conceptual changes visible in the handoff. Complete bracketed author requests. Prefer one clear qualification at the point of inference to repeated defensive disclaimers. Keep detailed counterarguments and research status in source notes and the claim audit; retain the hardest evidence in the reader-facing argument as well.

**Clarity without compression — established v3 direction carried into v4:** there is no word-count or reduction target. A shorter sentence is not automatically a clearer exposition. Preserve the connecting and grounding language through which a thought unfolds, including sentences that explain why a comparison matters. Distinguish repetition from a purposeful return: the same concern may recur in Jesus’s speech, the interpreters’ judgments, and our conduct, each time doing different work. Use v3-alt and the reviews to locate ambiguities and useful connections, not as a replacement voice or a template to blend mechanically into v4. Add a needed bridge before cutting a developed passage. Preserve the opening’s gradual movement, the early reason for comparative inquiry, “This is not a choice between logic and insight,” the positive account of humanity and divinity, and the final question about what might become possible through listening. Correct a referent or source detail locally rather than rebuilding its paragraph by default.

**Approved refinement after `c9c6387`:** keep the comparative introduction’s movement from a reason for cross-tradition inquiry through Gospel examples, Jesus’s Jewish inheritance, an Islamic voice within the Abrahamic inheritance, and Buddhist practice. This is a narrative order, not a hierarchy or a claim of historical influence. The conclusion should express what the inquiry has made more visible—hearing, continuation, responsibility, care—rather than return chiefly to the opening’s wording or another list of unresolved limits. Retain the final question, including “perhaps ourselves.”

### Narrative and contemplative direction

Let the human scene lead. Establish the flow of Mark before abstract categories: God’s reign, healing, disputed forgiveness, encounters around meals and Sabbath, and the accusation about Jesus’s power. The promise of forgiveness may intensify the warning rhetorically; do not assert that it has no substantive meaning of its own. Rising urgency is a reading of the recorded sequence, not permission to invent an unrecorded refusal between Jesus’s argument and warning.

Prefer “clinging to certainty” or “attachment to a view” for the general habit previously called rigidity; use dogmatism for its doctrinal form. Biblical hardening language remains a specific comparison. Do not use fanaticism as a loose synonym for conviction or disagreement. Describe what people are doing before naming their condition.

Parable and koan-like inquiry should shape the encounter rather than arrive only as late comparisons. A difficult saying may question the one interpreting it; this is not a waiver of philology, history, reasoning, or ethical scrutiny. Reason can expose hidden premises, invalid inferences, and contradictions, and may also lead to the point where an inherited conceptual frame proves too narrow. Do not make single-level formal consistency the goal of scriptural interpretation when a text is working through paradox, reversal, or distinct levels of designation. Conversely, do not use “paradox” to make words mean anything. The Diamond Sutra comparison at ND-10 is a model of deliberate deconstruction of fixed notions, not evidence that Mark 3 is historically a Buddhist koan. The active draft must work in print: do not use collapsible sections or hide the close reading. Place a compact, complete language study after the narrative, then let interpretation grow out of it.

**October 19 author direction — hearing and deep looking:** Mark 4 is approached through questions about receiving a teaching, not made into a settled explanation of who is excluded or a problem whose goal is a closed logical solution. Keep its quotation of Isaiah visible as part of Jesus's human and Jewish transmission. Ask what such a quotation might do within a compassionate address; do not claim to know that irony, psychological refusal, or judicial concealment is its only meaning. Distinguish inability to understand from deliberate unwillingness. The disciples' questions, explanations, light, and growing seed deepen the inquiry. “Parabolic teaching can both reveal and expose an inability to hear” is a central formulation, not a license to diagnose every confused person. Deep looking concerns sustained attention to words, life, relationships, and effects through which insight changes conduct. Introduce koan practice as a form of contemplative inquiry, then use “contemplative engagement” where it allows the passage to flow without pretending all traditions have one practice.

Humanizing Jesus need not normalize away significance or divinity. Attend to growth, fatigue, prayer, anger, grief, relationships, and teaching in circumstances, with sources. Do not require moral error or a merely inspired-human Christology as the price of taking humanity seriously. Classical Christian teaching itself affirms genuine humanity; its fuller claims should be represented accurately rather than borrowed selectively.

For “only begotten” / monogenēs, distinguish John’s claim about Jesus from the constructive image that each person is loved as if an only child. The latter is not a defensible literal translation, an established original meaning, or a demonstrated historical source of John 3:16. The Buddhist mother-and-only-child simile concerns the cultivation of boundless care, not a statement about a creator God. These teachings may participate in a genuine comparison without being made to say the same thing. Keep this thread proportionate to the Mark inquiry. See `sources/humanity-and-provocative-teaching.md`.

Introduce teachers and interpreters from every tradition, not only Christian writers, with a brief role and sufficient era/place to orient a reader. Introduce Thích Nhất Hạnh himself before relying on his thought. Avoid repeating biographies at every return. A named text or canonical collection can have its own brief orientation. Linji comparisons and a claim that TNH used a particular simile require a checked source for exact attribution; the attested spoon/tongue comparison is Dhammapada 64–65 until a specific TNH passage is located.

### Inquiry across traditions

Buddhist teachings are substantive resources for this author, not an intrusion requiring apology or Christian authorization. Introduce the relevant teaching, explain what it helps us see, and return to the passage. Neither tradition owns truth; neither our experience nor a claim to nonduality is exempt from examination. Attribute sources and distinguish a constructive comparison from a claim about ancient influence or the Greek text. Do not turn this into “dogmatic Christianity versus enlightened Buddhism.”

Keep additions proportionate. Prefer a few developed connections over a survey of Buddhist philosophy or a catalogue of Gospel parallels. Nonduality must not erase differences, harm, personal responsibility, or protective boundaries. A parable may be approached in a koan-like manner without claiming that it historically belongs to the Zen genre. Islamic sources contribute through listening, the manner of religious address, and a changed relation to past deeds. Al-Ghazālī’s Sufi account has the focused task of distinguishing instruction from lived transformation. These are later comparative voices, not substitutes for Jesus's Jewish setting or evidence of influence on him. Do not balance traditions by numerical quotas or flatten divine mercy, karmic result, and reconciliation into one doctrine.

Read the Third Mindfulness Training as a whole: freedom from imposed belief accompanies helping transform narrowness through compassionate dialogue. Do not equate an authentic warning about real danger with pressure to submit. Urgency, unpleasantness, and coercion are different matters. Conversely, calling speech a compassionate warning does not establish its truth or benefit. Attend to clear seeing, timing, the lives addressed, and effects. The focused comparison in MN 58 supports truthful and beneficial speech that can be unwelcome; retain that discourse’s severe context and do not treat it as authorization for any teacher’s asserted insight.

**Temporal vocabulary and continuation:** the histories of *aiōn* and *ʿālam* are interesting in their own right and need not be introduced only to refute a finite-duration reading. Ask what it means for something to last, what remains, and what changes. Preserve the idioms' conventional force without repeatedly treating every exploration of time as an attempted escape from “never.” Neither causal continuation nor impermanence establishes that every karmic result endures infinitely; neither change nor nonduality erases what happened. Passover, Augustine, John, Ezekiel, and Qur'anic passages can illuminate different relations to the past without becoming one Abrahamic account or a proof of Buddhist metaphysics.

## Evidence and quoted sources

Distinguish: (1) textual observation; (2) lexical or grammatical analysis; (3) historical reconstruction; (4) reception history; (5) constructive theology; (6) comparison with Buddhism; (7) personal testimony. Label uncertain claims rather than smoothing them into facts.

### Let the reader hear the evidence

Aaron prefers a complete relevant saying, verse, or coherent passage when the essay substantively engages a source, not isolated phrases that leave most of the meaning to our explanation. “Complete” concerns the relevant unit of thought, not an entire chapter. This applies across scripture, Augustine, Chrysostom, Aquinas, Calvin, Vatican documents, historical scholarship, Islamic and Sufi sources, Thích Nhất Hạnh, and Plum Village teachings. Introduce the speaker and setting, give the passage with its source and translation, then develop its contribution. Brief excerpts may still suffice for a passing reference. Context and quotation rights, not a word-count reduction goal, govern the choice; do not assemble unrelated fragments into an invented saying.

For supporting scripture, normally use exact NIV (2011) wording with book, chapter, verse, and edition. Keep the full italicized NIV Mark 3:28–29 lead-in. Do not silently mix NIV with another English edition: NIV Mark 3:30 says “impure spirit”; “unclean spirit” belongs in an explicitly labeled study rendering. Preserve punctuation, mark omissions with an ellipsis where needed, and bracket supplied words. Identify the prayer or setting when otherwise unclear; for example, the Lord’s Prayer in Matthew 6:12 and Luke 11:4. Give enough narrative around a quotation to let an unfamiliar reader understand it. New Qur’anic quotations identify their translation separately; a project-supplied rendering must never be attributed to a published translator.

**Close Greek, print-visible — current author preference:** for Mark 3:28, 3:29, and 3:30, give each complete verse in Greek, followed by romanization and a close literal English rendering. Keep useful explanatory notes but omit duplicate natural-English verse restatements; the NIV lead-in already supplies a continuous English reading. This supersedes the earlier requirement to repeat an additional English alternative beneath every verse. Use “Literal English,” not the specialist label “gloss,” in the essay. Brief explanatory brackets should follow Greek, romanization, meaning; they must not conceal interpretive additions. Do not use HTML details/summary or other screen-dependent presentation. More detailed word-by-word analysis and alternative translations remain in the dossiers.

**Parallel Syriac presentation:** retain the same pattern for Peshitta Mark 3:29–30: Syriac, romanization, literal English with critical words identified in brackets. Identify the Peshitta and displayed edition before the quotation, the omission of vowel marks, and the responsibility for the English translation. Full attested Syriac text is not a reconstruction. Romanization is a reading aid, not Jesus’s recovered pronunciation. Keep the order of the verse clear: Spirit of holiness, forgiveness, temporal phrase, judgment; verse 30 then supplies the impure-spirit accusation. Do not discuss the ending before the reader has seen what leads to it. See `sources/listening-sufi-and-syriac-text.md`.

Call this the Greek text of the Gospel of Mark, not an original transcript of Jesus. The Gospel is generally understood to have been composed in Greek; this does not settle the language or precise wording of the earlier encounter. Name the critical edition. For other scriptural passages, an English quotation normally suffices. Add a Greek term only when its contextual meaning materially advances the argument. “Being held answerable” often serves ordinary prose better than “liability,” while preserving the latter as a valid lexical term in source notes. Explain once that a “study rendering” is a translation supplied for this project, not an unidentified published translation.

For other sources, give author, work, and a stable locator such as homily and section, article and response, or numbered paragraph. Preserve the source’s wording; identify translations rather than implying the ancient author wrote the English. Distinguish an author’s conclusion from a quoted objection, a narrated speaker, or a translator’s note. Quote enough to support the claim without detaching a hopeful line from punitive or polemical context. Keep excerpts proportionate, respect applicable quotation limits, and avoid duplicating long excerpts between the draft and dossiers.

Never invent a quotation to satisfy this preference. If the actual text is unavailable, identify the summary or acquisition lead and defer the direct quotation. An abstract, catalog entry, or recording description is not the full work or a transcript. Before circulation, check quotations against the named edition and locator. Preserve the NIV acknowledgment and required source attribution.

### Source records and semantic care

The active publication draft uses footnotes and a bibliography; the research dossiers use stable source IDs and relative Markdown links. Keep the [publication source map](notes/publication-source-map.md) current. Reader-facing prose should identify the author or passage rather than relying on opaque IDs alone. Each source record needs an author or responsible institution, title, URL, stable locator where available, access status, a short verified quotation when useful, context, and limits. Never quote a recording from an editorial description as though the speaker said those exact words. Date-sensitive source checks in the initial deposit were made on 2026-10-10; later checks must identify their own scope.

A dictionary’s full semantic range is not the meaning of every occurrence. Do not derive a philosophy of time from the absence of chronos, or translate eis ton aiōna as “until the end of the age.” Preserve the force of the negative idiom and the difficulty of Matthew 12:32. Separate a bounded lexical discussion from a historical trajectory requiring dated evidence.

## Argumentative safeguards

Do not turn ancient Jewish religion into legalism and Christianity into its compassionate replacement. Do not equate Gospel narrative accusers with Jewish people generally. Forgiveness and judgment are present within Jewish scripture. Do not infer that someone knows inwardly that Jesus is right unless the particular source supplies that premise. Samaritans should not become generic representatives of a wholly unrelated religion; attend to the shared Israelite inheritance and the particular boundary in each story.

Keep Paul’s own letters distinct from Acts and 1 Timothy. Paul is a canonical test case for transformation, not a demonstrated instance of the exact act in Mark 3:29. Persecution, not Pharisaic identity, carries the moral weight of that comparison. Humanization must not become either an idealized Jesus protected from every difficult saying or an unsupported declaration that Jesus made particular mistakes.

Preserve the hardest evidence against the preferred interpretation. “Only while refusal continues” is constructive interpretation, not a hidden Greek clause. Mark’s present tense does not cancel its temporal qualification. “Offense” is a possible gloss, not an additional noun in the sentence; legal associations are already ancient. A preventive reading must account for what the accusers are already saying in Mark 3:30. Mark 4:10–12 complicates an account of every parable as a transparent invitation to awaken.

Distinguish logical validity, the truth of premises, the level and adequacy of a conceptual description, and lived realization. Reason does not by itself require permanent personal essences or prohibit change, and an apparent contradiction may dissolve when terms are functioning at different levels or when a designation is exposed as non-exhaustive. Do not claim that all Buddhist traditions reject noncontradiction or share one alternative logic. The two truths, historical/ultimate dimensions, and nonduality require specific attribution; they are not interchangeable labels for two opinions we prefer to hold simultaneously. A changing person can still bear an enduring consequence: rejecting an immutable essence does not refute a judicial interpretation or prove forgiveness. Retain ordinary accountability. “Paradox” is a disciplined invitation to investigate what our categories miss, not a permission slip for contradiction in the same respect or for readings detached from the text.

Keep recognition, healing, divine forgiveness, reconciliation, restored trust, and removal of consequences distinct. An injured person does not owe reconciliation or renewed access. Severe speech is not justified merely by the speaker’s claimed loving intent, nor discredited simply because it is severe. Consider truthfulness, power, freedom of response, timing, and effects; do not diagnose disagreement, trauma, disability, honest doubt, or a protective boundary as refusal of the Spirit.

### GitHub connector access and safe publication

Adapted from the [Collatz coordination publication procedure](https://github.com/aaronksolomon/Collatz_Project/blob/main/docs/research/integer-admissibility/coordination/AGENTS.md#atomic-claim-and-publication). A read-only checkout or unavailable local Git authentication does not by itself mean GitHub publication is unavailable: check the connected GitHub tools for the authorized repository. This is an alternate supported transport, not permission to bypass an access denial, branch protection, or an approval requirement. Never put credentials in files, URLs, or messages.

Confirm the repository (`aaronksolomon/mark-3-29`), authorized branch, and exact changed paths before writing. Do not assume the local checkout is on the target branch. The publication-polish branch has been merged into `main` as active v4; the immediately prior v3 is preserved in `working-draft-v3-frozen.md` and `outline-v3-frozen.md`. New version changes require Aaron’s direction.

When normal file tools or local push are unavailable, use the connector's Git data route. Tool names below refer to the GitHub connector; inspect the current schemas rather than guessing arguments.

1. **Read current state.** Use `fetch` with `https://api.github.com/repos/aaronksolomon/mark-3-29/git/ref/heads/BRANCH` to record the current head SHA. Read `/git/commits/HEAD_SHA` for its tree SHA, then `/git/trees/TREE_SHA?recursive=1` to locate paths and blob SHAs. Check for truncated results. Read exact bytes with `fetch_file` at that commit or `fetch_blob` by SHA; inspect the applicable `AGENTS.md` hierarchy.
2. **Prepare only approved changes.** Preserve unrelated files and dirty work. With local Git, inspect `git status --short`, stage explicit paths only, and run `git diff --cached --name-status` and `git diff --cached --check`. Never use repository-wide staging, commit-all, or deletion of paths merely absent from a sparse checkout.
3. **Create and verify blobs.** Call `create_blob` for each changed file with `encoding="utf-8"`; retain and verify the returned SHA/content. Do not re-upload unchanged files.
4. **Build on the current tree.** Call `create_tree` with `base_tree_sha` from step 1 and only the intended entries in `tree_elements` (`path`, existing `mode`, `type="blob"`, new blob `sha`). Never build an incomplete tree from scratch. Inspect the resulting tree/diff for unexpected paths, modes, or deletions.
5. **Create one commit.** Call `create_commit` with that new `tree_sha` and the recorded head as `parent_sha`, without extra parents. Verify the parent and complete changed-file list before publication.
6. **Update safely.** Re-read the branch ref. If it still matches, call `update_ref` with the exact `branch_name`, new commit `sha`, `expected_sha` equal to the recorded head, and `force=false`. If the head changed or the lease is rejected, inspect the new head and reapply only the authorized changes to its current files, rebuilding the tree and commit. Never force an unexpected head or overwrite another person's changes.
7. **Verify delivery.** Re-read the remote ref, commit, and changed files; confirm the expected commit and content are present and only authorized paths changed. A created blob/tree/commit alone is not a published save. If the update response is lost or uncertain, inspect remote state before retrying. Report the verified commit URL and paths.
