# Research and editing conventions

## Purpose and voice

Aaron directs this writing project. The current direction is scholarly, readable, open-ended inquiry into Mark 3:28–30, semantic history, and the humanity of teachers and interpreters. Do not imitate Thích Nhất Hạnh’s literary voice. Do not turn the inquiry into a predetermined defense of universalism, inerrancy, or biblical error. Personal narrative is optional and author-controlled.

### Editorial voice — established by Aaron’s revisions

The essay is a shared inquiry, not a verdict delivered from outside the tradition. Prefer “we,” “our histories,” and questions about how we receive and live a teaching. Let Jesus, the evangelists, their opponents, later teachers, and ourselves remain human participants. Preserve Aaron’s movement from a particular saying toward perception, relationship, and practice.

Use clear, connected paragraphs with an unhurried but direct rhythm. “Look deeply,” “consider,” “touch,” and “become open” belong to Aaron’s vocabulary when they name a real act of attention; do not scatter them as decorative spiritual language. Avoid imitative cadences, chains of isolated one-line sentences, grand declarations, and repeated “perhaps.” A question should identify something genuinely unresolved, not disguise a settled accusation. State established observations directly; save question marks for real questions.

Distinguish strong conviction from dogmatism. Name harm and responsibility plainly without making a person or tradition the embodiment of blindness. Do not flatten anger, severity, compassion, and fallibility into mutually exclusive categories.

The current v2 emphasis is compassionate warning within a human encounter. Develop this as a qualified preferred hearing, not only an endlessly deferred question. Distinguish what the saying asserts, what speaking it may seek to accomplish, and what ultimately happens to its hearers. Compassionate purpose does not prove reversibility or excuse intimidation. Claims about Jesus’s historical intention remain proportionate to their evidence. A principled contrary reading must not be diagnosed as dogmatism.

Polish locally before rewriting whole sections, unless Aaron explicitly requests a major revision. V2 is such a revision: move and connect strong v1 paragraphs before replacing them. Keep author edits unless accuracy, grammar, or coherence requires a change; make material conceptual changes visible in the handoff. Complete bracketed author requests. Prefer one clear qualification at the point of inference to repeated defensive disclaimers. Keep detailed counterarguments and research status in source notes and the claim audit; retain the hardest evidence in the reader-facing argument as well.

### Inquiry across traditions

Buddhist teachings are substantive resources for this author, not an intrusion requiring apology or Christian authorization. Introduce the relevant teaching, explain what it helps us see, and return to the passage. Neither tradition owns truth; neither our experience nor a claim to nonduality is exempt from examination. Attribute sources and distinguish a constructive comparison from a claim about ancient influence or the Greek text. Do not turn this into “dogmatic Christianity versus enlightened Buddhism.”

Keep additions proportionate. Prefer a few developed connections over a survey of Buddhist philosophy or a catalogue of Gospel parallels. Nonduality must not erase differences, harm, personal responsibility, or protective boundaries. A parable may be approached in a koan-like manner without claiming that it historically belongs to the Zen genre. Let Buddhist teaching question the preferred reading too: the Third Training’s concern about coercion is relevant to claims of compassionate severity.

## Evidence and quoted sources

Distinguish: (1) textual observation; (2) lexical or grammatical analysis; (3) historical reconstruction; (4) reception history; (5) constructive theology; (6) comparison with Buddhism; (7) personal testimony. Label uncertain claims rather than smoothing them into facts.

### Let the reader hear the evidence

Aaron prefers a brief quotation or short excerpt when the essay substantively engages a source, not merely a reference followed by our summary. This applies across the work: scripture, Augustine, Chrysostom, Aquinas, Calvin, Vatican documents, historical scholarship, Thích Nhất Hạnh, and Plum Village teachings. Introduce the speaker or responsible institution, show the relevant words, then explain the contribution and its limits. One well-chosen excerpt may anchor a connected discussion; do not quote every passing citation or accumulate quotations without interpretation.

For supporting scripture, normally use a short exact NIV (2011) excerpt with book, chapter, verse, and edition. Keep the full italicized NIV Mark 3:28–29 lead-in. Do not silently mix NIV with another English edition: for example, NIV Mark 3:30 says “impure spirit”; “unclean spirit” may be used in an explicitly labeled study rendering. Preserve punctuation, mark omissions with an ellipsis where needed, and bracket any supplied words. Do not fabricate a composite saying from related passages.

For the passage under close examination, give the named English translation, Greek script, romanization, a segmented literal gloss, and natural English alternatives. For other scriptural passages, the brief English quotation normally suffices. Add a Greek term only when its contextual meaning materially advances the argument. This follows Aaron’s scripture-format revision at `6370787`; do not restore extended Greek apparatus at every supporting reference.

For other sources, give author, work, and a stable locator such as homily and section, article and response, or numbered paragraph. Preserve the source’s wording; identify translations rather than implying the ancient author wrote the English. Distinguish an author’s conclusion from a quoted objection, a narrated speaker, or a translator’s note. Quote enough to support the claim without detaching a hopeful line from punitive or polemical context. Keep excerpts brief, respect applicable quotation limits, and avoid duplicating long excerpts between the draft and dossiers.

Never invent a quotation to satisfy this preference. If the actual text is unavailable, identify the summary or acquisition lead and defer the direct quotation. An abstract, catalog entry, or recording description is not the full work or a transcript. Before circulation, check every quotation against the named edition and locator. Preserve the NIV acknowledgment and any required source attribution.

### Source records and semantic care

Cite source IDs by relative Markdown links to topic files. Reader-facing prose should identify the author or passage rather than relying on opaque IDs alone. Each source record needs an author or responsible institution, title, URL, stable locator where available, access status, a short verified quotation when useful, context, and limits. Never quote a recording from an editorial description as though the speaker said those exact words. Date-sensitive source checks in the initial deposit were made on 2026-10-10.

A dictionary’s full semantic range is not the meaning of every occurrence. Do not derive a philosophy of time from the absence of chronos, or translate eis ton aiōna as “until the end of the age.” Preserve the force of the negative idiom and the difficulty of Matthew 12:32. Separate a bounded lexical discussion from a historical trajectory requiring dated evidence.

## Argumentative safeguards

Do not turn ancient Jewish religion into legalism and Christianity into its compassionate replacement. Do not equate Gospel narrative opponents with Jewish people generally. Forgiveness and judgment are present within Jewish scripture. Do not infer that an opponent knows inwardly that Jesus is right unless the particular source supplies that premise. Samaritans should not become generic representatives of a wholly unrelated religion; attend to the shared Israelite inheritance and the particular boundary in each story.

Keep Paul’s own letters distinct from Acts and 1 Timothy. Paul is a canonical test case for transformation, not a demonstrated instance of the exact act in Mark 3:29. Persecution, not Pharisaic identity, carries the moral weight of that comparison. Humanization must not become either an idealized Jesus protected from every difficult saying or an unsupported declaration that Jesus made particular mistakes.

Preserve the hardest evidence against the preferred interpretation. “Only while refusal continues” is constructive interpretation, not a hidden Greek clause. Mark’s present tense does not cancel its temporal qualification. “Offense” is a possible gloss, not an additional noun in the sentence; legal associations are already ancient. A preventive reading must account for what the accusers are already saying in Mark 3:30. Mark 4:10–12 complicates an account of every parable as a transparent invitation to awaken.

Distinguish logical validity, the truth of premises, the adequacy of a conceptual description, and lived realization. Logic does not by itself require fixed personal essences or prohibit change. Do not claim that all Buddhist traditions reject noncontradiction or share one alternative logic. The two truths, historical/ultimate dimensions, and nonduality require specific attribution; they are not interchangeable labels for two contradictory opinions. A changing person can still bear an enduring consequence: rejecting an immutable essence does not refute a judicial interpretation or prove forgiveness. Retain ordinary accountability rather than assigning it to a supposedly disposable lower truth.

Keep recognition, healing, divine forgiveness, reconciliation, restored trust, and removal of consequences distinct. An injured person does not owe reconciliation or renewed access. Severe speech is not justified merely by the speaker’s claimed loving intent. Consider truthfulness, power, freedom of response, and effects; do not diagnose disagreement, trauma, disability, honest doubt, or a protective boundary as refusal of the Spirit.

## Archives, privacy, and authorship

The repository is public. Add no new personal incidents, identifying community names, third-party names, or speculation about harm. Do not retrieve unrelated private correspondence. Assistant-composed first-person prose is not independent evidence about Aaron’s life. Clearly mark user writing versus assistant writing versus editorial summaries. Preserve archival errors with a warning and link to the claim audit; do not silently rewrite history. Archives are selective captures of the supplied thread, not a complete platform export.

## Repository discipline

Prefer ordinary UTF-8 Markdown and small text files. No binaries, full copyrighted texts, font files, credentials, or generated dependency trees. Keep quotations brief and attributed. No license change without Aaron’s decision. Read the current branch before a write; preserve others’ work; never force-push over an unexpected head. Verify that preserved v1 documents and archives are absent from the change set when updating v2.
