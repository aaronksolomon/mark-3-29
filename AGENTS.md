# Research and editing conventions

## Purpose and voice

Aaron directs this writing project. The current direction is scholarly, readable, open-ended inquiry into Mark 3:28–30, semantic history, and the humanity of teachers and interpreters. Do not imitate Thích Nhất Hạnh’s literary voice. Do not turn the inquiry into a predetermined defense of universalism, inerrancy, or biblical error. Personal narrative is optional and author-controlled.

### Editorial voice — established by Aaron’s revision `6801a0b`

The essay is a shared inquiry, not a verdict delivered from outside the tradition. Prefer “we,” “our histories,” and questions about how we receive and live a teaching. Let Jesus, the evangelists, their opponents, later teachers, and ourselves remain human participants. Preserve Aaron’s movement from a particular saying toward perception, relationship, and practice.

Use clear, connected paragraphs with an unhurried but direct rhythm. “Look deeply,” “consider,” “touch,” and “become open” belong to Aaron’s vocabulary when they name a real act of attention; do not scatter them as decorative spiritual language. Avoid imitative cadences, chains of isolated one-line sentences, grand declarations, and repeated “perhaps.” A question should identify something genuinely unresolved, not disguise a settled accusation.

Distinguish strong conviction from dogmatism. Name harm and responsibility plainly without making a person or tradition the embodiment of blindness. Preserve the possibility that a severe saying could be a compassionate warning, as a question rather than a recovered intention. Do not flatten anger, severity, compassion, and fallibility into mutually exclusive categories.

Polish locally before rewriting whole sections. Keep author edits unless accuracy, grammar, or coherence requires a change; make material conceptual changes visible in the handoff. Complete bracketed author requests. Prefer one clear qualification at the point of inference to repeated defensive disclaimers. Keep detailed counterarguments and research status in source notes and the claim audit; do not conceal the hardest evidence in either place.

### Inquiry across traditions

Buddhist teachings are substantive resources for this author, not an intrusion requiring apology or Christian authorization. Introduce the relevant teaching, explain what it helps us see, and return to the passage. Neither tradition owns truth; neither our experience nor a claim to nonduality is exempt from examination. Attribute sources and distinguish a constructive comparison from a claim about ancient influence or the Greek text. Do not turn this into “dogmatic Christianity versus enlightened Buddhism.”

Keep additions proportionate. Prefer a few developed connections over a survey of Buddhist philosophy or a catalogue of Gospel parallels. Nonduality must not erase differences, harm, personal responsibility, or protective boundaries. A parable may be approached in a koan-like manner without claiming that it historically belongs to the Zen genre.

## Start here

Read `README.md`, `essay/outline.md`, `notes/claim-audit.md`, and the relevant topic source file. `essay/working-draft.md` is the single active draft. Keep the outline and draft distinct; do not multiply competing canonical drafts.

## Evidence

Distinguish: (1) textual observation; (2) lexical or grammatical analysis; (3) historical reconstruction; (4) reception history; (5) constructive theology; (6) comparison with Buddhism; (7) personal testimony. Label uncertain claims rather than smoothing them into facts.

For significant Greek, give Greek script, romanization, a segmented literal gloss, and natural English alternatives. A dictionary’s full semantic range is not the meaning of every occurrence. Do not derive a philosophy of time from the absence of chronos, or translate eis ton aiōna as “until the end of the age.” Preserve the force of the negative idiom and the difficulty of Matthew 12:32.

Cite source IDs by relative Markdown links to topic files. Each source record needs an author or responsible institution, title, URL, stable locator where available, access status, a short verified quotation when useful, context, and limits. Never treat a catalog page or an abstract as a book/article fully read. Never quote a recording from an editorial description as though the speaker said those exact words. Date-sensitive source checks in the initial deposit were made on 2026-10-10.

## Argumentative safeguards

Do not turn ancient Jewish religion into legalism and Christianity into its compassionate replacement. Do not equate Gospel narrative opponents with Jewish people generally. Forgiveness and judgment are present within Jewish scripture. Do not infer that an opponent knows inwardly that Jesus is right unless the particular source supplies that premise. Samaritans should not become generic representatives of a wholly unrelated religion; attend to the shared Israelite inheritance and the particular boundary in each story.

Keep Paul’s own letters distinct from Acts and 1 Timothy. Paul is a canonical test case for transformation, not a demonstrated instance of the exact act in Mark 3:29. Humanization must not become either an idealized Jesus protected from every difficult saying or an unsupported declaration that Jesus made particular mistakes.

Preserve the hardest evidence against the preferred interpretation. “Only while refusal continues” is constructive interpretation, not a hidden Greek clause. Mark’s present tense does not cancel its temporal qualification. “Offense” is a possible gloss, not an additional noun in the sentence; legal associations are already ancient.

Distinguish logical validity, the truth of premises, the adequacy of a conceptual description, and lived realization. Logic does not by itself require fixed personal essences or prohibit change. Do not claim that all Buddhist traditions reject noncontradiction or share one alternative logic. The two truths, historical/ultimate dimensions, and nonduality require specific attribution; they are not interchangeable labels for two contradictory opinions. Retain ordinary accountability rather than assigning it to a supposedly disposable lower truth.

## Archives, privacy, and authorship

The repository is public. Add no new personal incidents, identifying community names, third-party names, or speculation about harm. Do not retrieve unrelated private correspondence. Assistant-composed first-person prose is not independent evidence about Aaron’s life. Clearly mark user writing versus assistant writing versus editorial summaries. Preserve archival errors with a warning and link to the claim audit; do not silently rewrite history. Archives are selective captures of the supplied thread, not a complete platform export.

## Repository discipline

Prefer ordinary UTF-8 Markdown and small text files. No binaries, full copyrighted texts, font files, credentials, or generated dependency trees. Keep quotations brief and attributed. No license change without Aaron’s decision. Use Git history for future revisions instead of saving every edit as a new file. Read the current branch before a write; preserve others’ work; never force-push over an unexpected head.
