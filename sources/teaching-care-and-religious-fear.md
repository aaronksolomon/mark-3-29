## TC-02 — Age-language and the Peshitta’s historical location

**William D. Mounce, online Greek dictionary:** [aiōnios](https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/aionios) and [aiōn](https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/aion). The inflected adjective *aiōniou* in Mark 3:29 is genitive singular, agreeing with *hamartēmatos*. Its derivation from *aiōn* does not involve a negative prefix meaning ageless, nor is it literally a multiword formula meaning from age to age. The main text identifies the relationship while leaving its contextual temporal force intact.

**Gorgias Encyclopedic Dictionary of the Syriac Heritage, “Peshitta”**, [entry](https://gedsh.bethmardutho.org/Peshitta), opening and section “Origin and Dating of the NT Peshitta.” The New Testament version became standard around 400 CE through a process involving Old Syriac material and revision toward Greek. Standardization is not a precise date for a single translator or surviving manuscript; the Old Testament translation has a different history. The main text now supplies this orientation before the existing Syriac quotation. The Syriac consonantal text and reading aids were not newly collated in this pass.

**Matthew 12:31–32, NIV**, [display](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+12%3A31-32&version=NIV). The final clause of verse 32 is quoted as a continuous relevant unit. The preceding distinction involving the Son of Man is supplied in the prose; the block is not represented as the complete two-verse parallel.

## TC-04 — Rūmī and the teacher who needs correction

**Jalāl al-Dīn Rūmī, Mathnawī, book II, “Moses and the Shepherd,” translated by Ibrahim Gamard from Persian.** [Part one, II.1720–1749](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/n.a-II-1720.html); [part two, II.1750–1764](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/n.a-II-1750.html); [continuation](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/n.a-II-1772.html). The story and the translator’s contextual notes were inspected. The quoted question is II.1751 in the running verse sequence, also checked against the [Persian/Nicholson display of II.1748–1757](https://www.masnavi.net/1/10/eng/2/1748/). Some footnote labels on Gamard’s page are offset; the running text and the named passage identify the quotation unambiguously.

The shepherd’s bodily images of care provoke Moses’s rebuke; the shepherd leaves in distress, and the divine address questions Moses’s separating action. Later developments complicate a simple opposition between right doctrine and warm feeling. The main text asks what correction does within a life, not whether every religious utterance is equally adequate.

This is Rūmī’s Sufi teaching story, not a historical report about Moses or a passage from the Torah or Qur’an. Do not turn it into harsh Judaism versus compassionate Islam, or detach Rūmī from his Islamic commitments. Al-Ghazālī remains in the essay: knowing and living, and the effects of a teacher’s correction, are distinct contributions.

**Note:** n70.

This is a small extension of the essay’s concern with descriptions that prevent another person from being heard, not a new organizing theme.
