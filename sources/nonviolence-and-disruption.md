# Nonviolence and disruption

**Checked:** October 14, 2026. This dossier supports a constructive comparison, not a claim that Walter Wink directly interprets Mark 3:28–30.

## NV-01 — Wink on loving confrontation

- **Source:** Walter Wink, *Jesus and Nonviolence: A Third Way* (Minneapolis: Fortress Press, 2003), ch. 2, pp. 9–37, especially 25–28.
- **Access:** [Consulted chapter scan](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/553ea282e4b0e1b549d403d5/t/69a7ca048b966946293857c8/1772603908810/JFP_Walter%2BWink_Nonviolent%2BJesus_Chapter%2B2.pdf); [edition record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/249234555); [publisher retrospective confirming publication](https://ms.fortresspress.com/downloads/9780800699871Intro.pdf).
- **Status:** Relevant chapter inspected; printed p. 26 visually checked. The essay quotes its short sentence beginning “Loving confrontation”; the quotation is not duplicated here.
- **Context:** Wink interprets Matthew 5’s cheek, garment, and second-mile sayings as active resistance rather than submission or violent retaliation. At p. 26 he warns against both vindictive humiliation and sentimental niceness. His point concerns those resisting injustice as well as the possibility of the wrongdoer’s change.
- **Limits:** This is not direct Mark 3 exegesis. The essay does not adopt his whole historical reconstruction or his lexical argument about *antistēnai*. No original intention or successful effect of Mark’s warning is proved by the comparison.

## NV-02 — Nonviolent pressure is not simple noncoercion

- **Source:** Walter Wink, “Beyond Just War and Pacifism: Jesus’ Nonviolent Way.” [CRES text](https://www.cres.org/star/~wink.htm), especially “Go the Second Mile,” “Jesus’ Third Way,” and “Beyond Just War and Pacifism.”
- **Status:** Online primary essay inspected; the host explicitly states author permission. A same-title article appears in *Review & Expositor* 89, no. 2 (1992): 197–214, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/003463739208900204), but identity of every online paragraph with the journal printing was not verified. Cite online sections, not an invented journal pinpoint.
- **Context and limit:** Wink permits nonviolent pressure that forces unwelcome decisions. Do not equate his view with noncoercion or collapse it into the Third Mindfulness Training’s rejection of imposed beliefs. Enemy-love remains integral to his argument. No additional direct quotation is needed.

## NV-03 — Addressees and possible wider effects in Mark

- **Primary text:** Mark 3:20–35, especially 20, 22–23, and 31–32; [USCCB text](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/mark/3).
- **Observation:** Jesus summons the scribes who have made the accusation. The narrative frames the encounter with a gathered crowd.
- **Interpretive proposal:** A warning addressed to accusers may also challenge the accusation’s authority among witnesses and protect their openness to healing. This possibility follows the author’s October 14 revision; it is not a separately reported narrative event.
- **Limits:** Do not claim everyone hears the warning, that Jesus explicitly redirects it to everyone, or that the speech succeeds. Do not label all harsh speech nonviolent or compassionate because its author says it is. Its truth, use of power, consequences, and care for people affected remain open to examination. The negative temporal idiom and possible finality are not weakened by the proposed function.
