## Reading the status labels

- **Text checked:** the relevant written passage was inspected in the linked online edition. This does not claim a critical edition in the original language was consulted.
- **Excerpt checked:** a specified excerpt, rather than the entire work, was inspected.
- **Abstract/bibliography checked:** the article’s landing page was read; the full argument has not been assessed.
- **Catalog checked:** title/author/description or selected-edition details were confirmed. This is not a claim to have read the book.
- **Recording description checked:** the hosting institution’s summary was inspected, not the audio. Quote the summary as editorial text, not as the teacher’s exact speech.
- **Lead only:** retained from the conversation or a bibliography; locate and verify before using as evidence.
- **Study reconstruction:** project-composed language, not a quotation from an ancient witness. A plausible sentence does not establish that Jesus spoke it.

Source notes separate what a text says from what the project proposes to infer. When a source supports both a hopeful and a severe interpretation, retain both. Source IDs remain stable even if files grow. New access checks do not imply that all existing sources were freshly reviewed in full.

## Quotation and translation conventions

A substantive discussion should normally let the reader encounter a complete relevant verse, saying, or coherent unit before our interpretation. This is not a demand to reproduce whole chapters or exceed quotation limits. Give the speaker, setting, translation, and locator; brief phrases still serve passing references where they do not conceal context.

English patristic quotations are translations, not the Greek or Latin originals. Biblical English supplied as a study rendering is our own unless an edition is named. In the active essay, the full Greek of Mark 3:28–30 is followed by romanization and literal English with brief clarifications. Useful explanatory notes remain; duplicate natural-English restatements have been removed at Aaron's request, since the full NIV lead-in supplies a continuous reading. The Greek remains visible in ordinary Markdown suitable for print. More detailed word-by-word notes and translation alternatives remain in the dossiers.

The Syriac section now follows the same pattern for Peshitta Mark 3:29–30. The original-language text is attested; the English is a project translation. Romanization and omission of vowel marks are identified as presentation choices, not reconstructed first-century speech. See [LS-04](listening-sufi-and-syriac-text.md#ls-04--complete-peshitta-verses-and-reading-aids).

The Mishnah and Maimonides snippets are explicitly identified as translations supplied for the project from the linked Hebrew, not quotations from an unconsulted published English edition. Sirach is quoted in NRSVUE, not NIV. Qur’an 39:18 is in Saheeh International; the existing 25:70 excerpt remains in Mustafa Khattab’s *The Clear Quran*. Links to online Bible displays are access points, not substitutes for a named critical text and apparatus. Some sites display English that does not match the Greek noun exactly; check independently. ## What has not been established

No full historical semantic trajectory has been demonstrated. No reconstruction of Jesus’s precise utterance is adopted. No universalist or irreversible-hardening conclusion is settled. The interbeing of time is a comparative resource, not an etymology of aiōn. Rigidity, now more often described as clinging to certainty, is an interpretive proposal, not a new translation of Mark 3:29. Nondual and koan-like readings concern how a teaching may be received; they do not establish Buddhist influence on the evangelists or make arbitrary interpretation permissible. Reasoning remains part of the inquiry, but it need not force every religious utterance into a single level of description.

The Jewish dossier is a starting corpus, not a single theology or a completed survey from antiquity to the present. Later rabbinic and medieval sources must not be projected into Jesus’s scene. A Gospel’s composition date is not the date of its oldest surviving copy. Mark 4’s language of concealment does not independently establish eternal duration. The current prose asks what quoting Isaiah might do in a compassionate address; it does not claim to have resolved the purpose clause or every theological account of understanding.

Age-language does not alone establish finite duration or a non-linear ancient worldview. Passover practice, Augustine’s account of memory and expectation, and John’s present eternal life provide specific comparisons, not one shared system or an automatic solution to Mark’s warning. [TA-01–TA-07](time-age-and-eternity.md#ta-01--aiōn-and-english-eon). The further inquiry into what lasts concerns continuation through changing conditions, not a claim that every consequence persists identically and infinitely. Ezekiel's and the Qur'an's accounts of changed deeds and divine mercy are not relabeled as Buddhist karma. See [DC-05–DC-08](deep-looking-and-continuation.md#dc-05--temporal-expressions-as-perspective-not-a-verdict).

Each person loved as if an only child is constructive theology, not a replacement translation or demonstrated historical origin of John’s monogenēs. The Linji and Sun My Heart records remain leads. The MN 58 comparison retains its severe context and does not certify a teacher’s claims merely because speech is called compassionate. See [HT-03–HT-08](humanity-and-provocative-teaching.md#ht-03), [ND-09](nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-09--truthful-beneficial-and-timely-speech), [ND-10](nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-10--reason-paradox-and-the-diamond-sutra), [JC-01–JC-11](jewish-context-and-cross-tradition-inquiry.md#jc-01), and the [claim audit](../notes/claim-audit.md).
