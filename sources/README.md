## Reading the status labels

- **Text checked:** the relevant written passage was inspected in the linked online edition. This does not claim a critical edition in the original language was consulted.
- **Excerpt checked:** a specified excerpt, rather than the entire work, was inspected.
- **Abstract/bibliography checked:** the article’s landing page was read; the full argument has not been assessed.
- **Catalog checked:** title/author/description or selected-edition details were confirmed. This is not a claim to have read the book.
- **Recording description checked:** the hosting institution’s summary was inspected, not the audio. Quote the summary as editorial text, not as the teacher’s exact speech.
- **Lead only:** retained from the conversation or a bibliography; locate and verify before using as evidence.
- **Study reconstruction:** project-composed language, not a quotation from an ancient witness. A plausible sentence does not establish that Jesus spoke it.

Source notes separate what a text says from what the project proposes to infer. When a source supports both a hopeful and a severe interpretation, retain both. Source IDs remain stable even if files grow. New access checks do not imply that all existing sources were freshly reviewed in full.

## Quotation and translation conventions

Brief direct quotations identify their author and locator. English patristic quotations are translations, not the Greek or Latin originals. Biblical English supplied as a study rendering is our own unless an edition is named. In the active essay, the full Greek of Mark 3:28–30 is followed by romanization, literal English with brief clarifications, and natural English alternatives. It is visible in ordinary Markdown suitable for print. More detailed word-by-word notes remain in the dossiers. A mechanical literal rendering is not automatically the best translation.

The Mishnah and Maimonides snippets are explicitly identified as project study renderings from the linked Hebrew, not quotations from an unconsulted published English edition. Sirach is quoted in NRSVUE, not NIV. Preserve these distinctions and obtain qualified review before publication.

Links to online Bible displays are access points, not substitutes for a named critical text and apparatus. Some sites display English that does not match the Greek noun exactly; check independently. A critical Greek edition is not a transcript of the historical Jesus. Full original-language and manuscript work remains in the research queue.

The new Syriac records distinguish the attested Peshitta from the earlier chat’s hybrid back-translation. The Sinaitic ending reported in that chat remains a collation task; the present pass checked the Peshitta independently at Dukhrana. Hebrew in AS-09 is explicitly a study exercise. Ritual Hebrew in a later rabbinic description does not establish the language of every first-century Temple conversation.

## What has not been established

No full historical semantic trajectory has been demonstrated. No reconstruction of Jesus’s precise utterance is adopted. No universalist or irreversible-hardening conclusion is settled. The interbeing of time is a comparative resource, not an etymology of aiōn. Rigidity, now more often described as clinging to certainty, is an interpretive proposal, not a new translation of Mark 3:29. Nondual and koan-like readings concern how a teaching may be received; they do not establish Buddhist influence on the evangelists or remove coherent reasoning.

The Jewish dossier is a starting corpus, not a single theology or a completed survey from antiquity to the present. Later rabbinic and medieval sources must not be projected into Jesus’s scene. A Gospel’s composition date is not the date of its oldest surviving copy. Mark 4’s hardening language does not independently establish eternal duration; its concealment and judgment still require examination.

Age-language does not alone establish finite duration or a non-linear ancient worldview. Passover practice, Augustine’s account of memory and expectation, and John’s present eternal life provide specific comparisons, not one shared system or an automatic solution to Mark’s warning. Each person loved as if an only child is constructive theology, not a replacement translation or demonstrated historical origin of John’s monogenēs. The Linji and Sun My Heart records remain leads. The MN 58 comparison retains its severe context and does not certify a teacher’s claims merely because speech is called compassionate.
