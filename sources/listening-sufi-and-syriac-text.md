# Listening, Sufi practice, and the Syriac text

**Scope:** October 19, 2026, approved refinement of active v4 from Aaron’s author revision `c9c6387c4cc14c4314725d7adc81c990a88e0c3e`. These records supplement [Deep looking, hearing, and continuation](deep-looking-and-continuation.md) and [Aramaic and Hebrew witnesses](semitic-gospel-witnesses.md). They do not replace their historical access records or make unconsulted works into sources.

The introduction adds a Qur’anic account of listening; section 4 adds one Sufi account of lived understanding; section 5 now gives a complete relevant verse about religious address. The Syriac section shows attested text and distinguishes it from the English translation supplied for the essay. These contributions have specific tasks, not a goal of equal numerical representation among traditions.

## LS-01 — Listening and the Abrahamic orientation

**Qur’an 39:18**, Saheeh International translation, read with 39:17. [Named translation display](https://quran.com/es/az-zumar/18/translations/en-sahih-international); [surrounding passage](https://quran.com/39/17-18). The first page has a Spanish interface but displays the identified English translation. The whole of verse 18 is quoted in the essay rather than duplicated here.

**Checked:** the displayed English wording and the setting in verse 17; not a critical Arabic edition or a comprehensive tafsir survey. The verse joins listening, following, divine guidance, and understanding. Its immediate subjects turn toward God. The essay’s contribution is the relation between listening and response, not a claim that the passage declares every religious teaching equally sound.

**Interpretive limit:** translations and their notes vary over how broadly to understand the speech heard—what is heard generally or particularly revealed teaching. See the [translation comparison](https://www.alim.org/quran/compare/surah/39/18/). The introduction retains this question in its note rather than presenting a modern pluralist reading as the only meaning.

**Qur’an 2:136:** [text](https://quran.com/2/136), inspected for its naming of Abraham, Moses, and Jesus. It supports the brief orientation to an Abrahamic prophetic inheritance. Shared inheritance does not imply identical understandings of prophecy, Jesus, or revelation, and no historical influence on Mark is asserted.

**Main-text note:** `n68`.

## LS-02 — Al-Ghazālī: knowing and undergoing

**Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī, The Confessions of Al Ghazzali**, translated by Claud Field (London: John Murray, 1909), “Sufism,” printed pp. 41–44. [Project Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/58977/58977-h/58977-h.htm).

**Checked:** title-page details, biographical orientation, the comparison with health and satiety on p. 41, and the account of examining his teaching motives on pp. 43–44. The relevant sentence is quoted completely in the essay; it is not repeated here. No Arabic critical edition was consulted, and this record does not certify the historical accuracy of every statement in Field’s introductory biography.

Al-Ghazālī distinguishes acquiring information about a condition from living it. His account joins Sufi doctrine with practice and moral transformation, and turns examination toward his own motives. It retains God, revelation, and judgment. The essay uses this to ask whether understanding reaches the person who understands, not to attribute an abandonment of religious conviction to him.

**Main-text note:** `n69`. The English wording belongs to Field’s identified translation, not to an untranslated medieval manuscript or a modern paraphrase.

## LS-03 — A complete unit of religious address

**Qur’an 16:125**, with 16:126–128 as context. [Arabic text and comparison of named translations](https://www.alim.org/quran/compare/surah/16/125/); [Quran.com display](https://quran.com/16/125).

**Checked:** displayed Arabic and translations by Marmaduke Pickthall and Mustafa Khattab. The essay now provides a complete English rendering of verse 125 supplied for this project. It is explicitly not quoted as either translator’s wording. Its clauses retain the invitation to the Lord’s way, wisdom and counsel, the manner of argument, and the divine knowledge of those who stray and those who are guided. No specialist certification or comprehensive commentary study is claimed.

The relevant unit makes clear what the invitation is to before the essay interprets how it should be offered. This is not a statement that every religious claim is equivalent. The subsequent context concerning retaliation and patience remains recorded in [DC-08](deep-looking-and-continuation.md#dc-08--islamic-counsel-concerning-speech-and-mercy).

**Main-text note:** `n65`, updated without changing the stable label. The earlier brief Khattab quotations have been replaced; the continuing Khattab quotation in Qur’an 25:70 retains its own attribution.

## LS-04 — Complete Peshitta verses and reading aids

**Peshitta, Mark 3:29–30**, British and Foreign Bible Society text displayed by Dukhrana Biblical Research, with George A. Kiraz’s SEDRA morphology.

- [Mark 3:29](https://www.dukhrana.com/peshitta/analyze_verse.php?font=Serto+Batnan&size=125%25&source=&verse=Mark+3%3A29)
- [Mark 3:30](https://www.dukhrana.com/peshitta/analyze_verse.php?font=Serto+Batnan&size=125%25&source=&verse=Mark+3%3A30)
- [GEDSH, “Peshitta”](https://gedsh.bethmardutho.org/Peshitta)
- [Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon, ʿlm](https://cal.huc.edu/oneentry.php?cits=all&lemma=%28lm+N)

**Checked:** both displayed Syriac verses, the relevant morphological entries, and the version’s orientation in GEDSH. This is a displayed-text comparison, not manuscript-image collation. The essay reproduces consonantal text with vowel/pronunciation marks and final verse punctuation omitted. It preserves the attested word sequence; it does not substitute the earlier chat’s conjectural sin-ending for the Peshitta’s judgment-ending.

**Presentation:** each verse has Syriac, simplified conventional romanization, and literal English supplied for the essay. Critical expressions are identified in the English brackets. The romanization is not a recovery of first-century Galilean pronunciation. “Literal” identifies a close reading aid, not the only adequate English translation.

The sequence is now visible before exposition: Spirit of holiness, no forgiveness, the temporal expression, judgment; verse 30 supplies the impure-spirit accusation. The source uses judgment-language at the end of verse 29. That is not an addition made by the project’s English translator. How this relates to a Greek exemplar, translation, and revision remains unresolved.

The note on *l-ʿālam* distinguishes the word-by-word age-expression from its conventional force. Greek *aiōn* and Aramaic *ʿālam* have different histories; their comparison concerns overlapping use and translation, not a common root. No endpoint or general ancient philosophy of time is inferred from etymology alone.

**Main-text notes:** `n22`, `n62`, with the existing `n23` for the Greek/Aramaic temporal comparison. Earlier [AS-04](semitic-gospel-witnesses.md#as-04--peshitta-mark-32830-checked-wording) and [DC-05](deep-looking-and-continuation.md#dc-05--temporal-expressions-as-perspective-not-a-verdict) remain useful context. No new Old Syriac Sinaitic collation is claimed.
