Important passages include Greek, romanization, a segmented gloss, and English alternatives. The full Bible is not reproduced here.

## B-02

**Mark 3:20–35, especially 3:28–30.**

- Greek: [3:28](https://www.greekbible.com/mark/3/28), [3:29](https://www.greekbible.com/mark/3/29), [3:30](https://www.greekbible.com/mark/3/30)
- Context: [Mark 3, SBLGNT display](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+3&version=SBLGNT)
- **Status:** core Greek checked; narrative context and translation problems cross-checked with [L-01](greek-and-semantic-history.md#l-01). The Online Greek Bible’s English at 3:29 says “condemnation” despite displaying hamartēmatos; do not copy that English as a literal match.

### Mark 3:28

**Greek:** Ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν ὅτι πάντα ἀφεθήσεται τοῖς υἱοῖς τῶν ἀνθρώπων, τὰ ἁμαρτήματα καὶ αἱ βλασφημίαι ὅσα ἐὰν βλασφημήσωσιν.

**Romanization:** Amēn legō hymin hoti panta aphethēsetai tois huiois tōn anthrōpōn, ta hamartēmata kai hai blasphēmiai hosa ean blasphēmēsōsin.

**Segmented gloss:** Amēn, truly/amen; legō hymin, I say to you; hoti, that; panta aphethēsetai, all things will be forgiven; tois huiois tōn anthrōpōn, to the children of human beings; ta hamartēmata kai hai blasphēmiai, the sins and the blasphemies; hosa ean blasphēmēsōsin, whatever they may utter as blasphemy.

**Study rendering:** “Truly I tell you: all will be forgiven human beings—their sins and whatever blasphemies they utter.”

**Ordinary English alternative:** “All human sins and blasphemies will be forgiven.” This condenses the syntax. It does not mean that panta grammatically designates all individual people. Read the immediately following exception.

### Mark 3:29

**Greek:** ὃς δ’ ἂν βλασφημήσῃ εἰς τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, οὐκ ἔχει ἄφεσιν εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα, ἀλλὰ ἔνοχός ἐστιν αἰωνίου ἁμαρτήματος.

**Romanization:** Hos d’ an blasphēmēsē eis to pneuma to hagion, ouk echei aphesin eis ton aiōna, alla enochos estin aiōniou hamartēmatos.

**Segmented gloss:** hos d’ an, but whoever; blasphēmēsē, should blaspheme/malign; eis to pneuma to hagion, against the Holy Spirit; ouk echei aphesin, does not have forgiveness/remission; eis ton aiōna, into/unto the age; alla enochos estin, but is guilty/liable; aiōniou hamartēmatos, of/for an eternal sin.

**Ordinary English:** “But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness, but is guilty of an eternal sin.”

**Alternative:** “But whoever maligns the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness forever, but is answerable for an eternal sin.”

“Into the age” is a mechanical gloss of an idiom. “Age-enduring” can be considered as a lexical study gloss of aiōnios, but it risks implying finite duration and is not the default contextual translation. Neither “until” nor “end” occurs here. “While refusal continues” is also absent: that is an interpretation to test, not recovered grammar.

### Mark 3:30

**Greek:** ὅτι ἔλεγον· Πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον ἔχει.

**Romanization:** Hoti elegon: pneuma akatharton echei.

**Segmented gloss:** hoti, because; elegon, they were saying; pneuma akatharton, an unclean spirit; echei, he has.

**Ordinary English:** “Because they were saying, ‘He has an unclean spirit.’” “They kept saying” may convey the imperfect in context; it does not add an explicit condition for subsequent forgiveness.

**Context and limits:** Verse 30 connects the warning to the accusation. The scribes are from Jerusalem; Matthew’s parallel names Pharisees. The wider Markan unit includes Jesus’s family and questions of belonging. Calling divine liberation demonic is the narrator’s evaluation; private motives and exact historical speech are not independently recovered by repeating that evaluation.

**Controls to study:** Mark 1:14–15; 2:1–12; 3:5; 4:10–12; 11:14,25; 14:64. These complicate simple mercy-versus-judgment and modern-versus-ancient legal contrasts.

## B-03

**Matthew 12:31–32; comparison with Matthew 28:20.**

- [Matthew 12, USCCB text and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/12)
- [Greek of Matthew 12:32](https://www.greekbible.com/matthew/12/32)
- [Greek of Matthew 28:20](https://www.greekbible.com/matthew/28/20)
- **Status:** Matthew 12 context checked. The 28:20 comparison is a textual control, not an argument about what happens after the endpoint.

**Greek:** οὐκ ἀφεθήσεται αὐτῷ οὔτε ἐν τούτῳ τῷ αἰῶνι οὔτε ἐν τῷ μέλλοντι.

**Romanization:** Ouk aphethēsetai autō oute en toutō tō aiōni oute en tō mellonti.

**Segmented gloss:** ouk aphethēsetai autō, it will not be forgiven to him; oute en toutō tō aiōni, neither in this age; oute en tō mellonti, nor in the coming one.

**Ordinary English:** “It will not be forgiven, either in this age or in the age to come.” Alternative: “Neither in the present age nor in the coming age.”

Compare **ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος**, *heōs tēs synteleias tou aiōnos*: heōs, until; tēs synteleias, the completion; tou aiōnos, of the age. Natural English: “until the end of the age.” Those additional words occur in Matthew 28:20, not Mark 3:29.

**Interpretive weight:** Matthew is a serious obstacle to a reading that promises forgiveness simply when the present age ends. A beyond-both-ages interpretation is a theological construction. Distinguish Matthew’s language from claims about its source history.

## B-05

**Galatians 1:8–16.**

- [USCCB chapter and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/galatians/1)
- **Status:** text checked.

Paul describes persecution, zeal for ancestral traditions, and revelation of the Son. These verses supply his own account of changed direction. Verses 8–9 also contain severe language against a different gospel: Paul should not be used as an uncomplicated embodiment of openness in contrast to supposedly harsh evangelists.

**Use:** a test of claims that destructive religious misrecognition cannot change. **Limit:** Paul does not say he attributed Jesus’s exorcisms to a demon.

## B-06

**Philippians 3:5–6.**

- [Greek of 3:5](https://www.greekbible.com/philippians/3/5)
- [Greek of 3:6](https://www.greekbible.com/philippians/3/6)
- **Status:** 3:5 text checked; 3:6 is the adjacent persecution reference to read with it.

**Greek:** κατὰ νόμον Φαρισαῖος — *kata nomon Pharisaios*.

**Segmented gloss:** kata nomon, as regards law; Pharisaios, a Pharisee.

**English:** “As to the law, a Pharisee,” or “with respect to the law, a Pharisee.”

This self-description does not identify Paul as one of the persons in Mark’s scene or justify generalizing about all Pharisees.

## B-07

**Acts 9:17–18.**

- [Greek of 9:17](https://www.greekbible.com/acts/9/17)
- [Greek of 9:18](https://www.greekbible.com/acts/9/18)
- **Status:** 9:17 checked; read the whole conversion narrative before further historical claims.

Ananias connects regaining sight with being filled with the Holy Spirit. This is Acts’ narrative about Paul, distinct from Paul’s own report in Galatians. Do not infer that physical blindness in general is a moral defect.

## B-08

**1 Timothy 1:12–16, with 1:20 as a control.**

- [USCCB chapter and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1timothy/1)
- [USCCB introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1timothy/0)
- **Status:** chapter text checked; introduction access was inconsistent. Consult a scholarly introduction for the contested authorship and dating rather than deriving either from this verse.

The letter portrays Paul as formerly a blasphemer, persecutor, and violent person who received mercy, explicitly mentioning ignorance and unbelief. That qualification must accompany use of the passage as a transformation example. The argument that Mark’s opponents knowingly rejected recognized truth is not explicitly stated by Mark. Neither does 1 Timothy prove that they were ignorant in Paul’s sense. At 1:20 the same chapter joins severe disciplinary action with the aim of learning not to blaspheme.

## B-09

**John 8:31–36.**

- [SBLGNT 8:31–32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+8%3A31-32&version=SBLGNT)
- [Greek 8:31](https://www.greekbible.com/john/8/31)
- [Greek 8:32](https://www.greekbible.com/john/8/32)
- **Status:** principal Greek checked in the SBLGNT display during setup.

**Greek:** Ἐὰν ὑμεῖς μείνητε ἐν τῷ λόγῳ τῷ ἐμῷ, ἀληθῶς μαθηταί μού ἐστε, καὶ γνώσεσθε τὴν ἀλήθειαν, καὶ ἡ ἀλήθεια ἐλευθερώσει ὑμᾶς.

**Romanization:** Ean hymeis meinēte en tō logō tō emō, alēthōs mathētai mou este, kai gnōsesthe tēn alētheian, kai hē alētheia eleutherōsei hymas.

**Segmented gloss:** ean hymeis meinēte, if you remain; en tō logō tō emō, in my word; alēthōs mathētai mou este, truly you are my disciples; kai gnōsesthe tēn alētheian, and you will know the truth; kai hē alētheia eleutherōsei hymas, and the truth will free you.

**English:** “If you remain in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.” “Continue in my teaching” is a natural alternative to “remain in my word.”

**Limit:** The clause says “are,” not “become.” The audience is described as having believed, and the following discussion concerns slavery to sin and freedom through the Son. Lived participation is a defensible reading; autonomous personal experience as the sole judge is not what the passage simply states.

## B-10

**John 7:17.**

- [Greek text](https://www.greekbible.com/john/7/17)
- **Status:** Greek checked.

**Greek:** ἐάν τις θέλῃ τὸ θέλημα αὐτοῦ ποιεῖν, γνώσεται περὶ τῆς διδαχῆς πότερον ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ ἐστιν ἢ ἐγὼ ἀπ’ ἐμαυτοῦ λαλῶ.

**Romanization:** Ean tis thelē to thelēma autou poiein, gnōsetai peri tēs didachēs poteron ek tou theou estin ē egō ap’ emautou lalō.

**Segmented gloss:** ean tis thelē, if anyone is willing; to thelēma autou poiein, to do his will; gnōsetai peri tēs didachēs, will know concerning the teaching; poteron ek tou theou estin, whether it is from God; ē egō ap’ emautou lalō, or I speak from myself.

**English study rendering:** “Anyone willing to do God’s will will recognize whether this teaching comes from God or whether I speak on my own.”

**Limit:** Do not omit willingness, God’s will, or the specific discernment about Jesus’s teaching. “Do, then know” is a compressed interpretation, not the complete verse.

## B-11

**John 3:21.**

- [Greek text](https://www.greekbible.com/john/3/21)
- **Status:** Greek checked.

**Greek:** ὁ δὲ ποιῶν τὴν ἀλήθειαν — *ho de poiōn tēn alētheian*.

**Segmented gloss:** ho de, but the one; poiōn, doing; tēn alētheian, the truth.

**English:** “But the one who does the truth,” or “who lives by the truth.” The full verse connects coming to the light with deeds wrought in God.

**Use:** ethical enactment belongs to the passage’s language. **Limit:** this is not merely a theory of testing propositions by subjective experience. Study the Semitic and Johannine usage before building a general epistemology.

## B-12

**John 9:1–3,39–41.**

- [USCCB text and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/9)
- **Status:** text checked.

The ending relates claiming to see to sin remaining. It is an important comparison for the proposed relation between certainty and misrecognition. The beginning refuses the disciples’ simple attribution of a man’s blindness to his or his parents’ sin. Preserve this protection against treating disability as culpable spiritual failure.
