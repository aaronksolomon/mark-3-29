## HT-01

**Mark 1–3: narrative setting and the reversal of blasphemy language.**

- [Mark 1, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+1&version=NIV)
- [Mark 2, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+2&version=NIV)
- [Mark 3, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+3&version=NIV)
- [Mark 3, NABRE and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/mark/3)
- **Status:** relevant chapter texts checked in this pass. This is a literary reading, not a reconstruction of an independently established historical sequence.
- **Key excerpts used in the draft:** the announcement of the kingdom in 1:15, the accusation of blasphemy in 2:7, and the summons and accusation in 3:22–30. See the draft for the brief quotations rather than duplicating them here.

**Narrative sequence:** Spirit, proclamation, exorcism, healing, and solitary prayer in chapter 1; forgiveness and disputed authority, meals with sinners, fasting, and Sabbath in chapter 2; Sabbath healing, anger and grief, crowds, the Twelve, family intervention, and the accusation from Jerusalem in chapter 3. The source-of-power controversy does not open chapter 3; the Sabbath-healing episode does.

**Interpretive use:** blasphemy is first attributed to Jesus for forgiving; the later warning brings blasphemy language to an accusation against his work. Different scenes and speakers must not be silently merged. The opening breadth of forgiveness may intensify the exception rhetorically without becoming empty language whose sole function is contrast.

**Limits:** Mark records no reply between 3:23–27 and 3:28–29. Rising urgency is a reading of the sequence, not a demonstrated failed reasoning attempt followed by a separately recorded escalation. Mark 3:23 introduces parabolic speech; 3:29 is a severe saying within the address, not necessarily itself a parable. No cited verse says the scribes previously pronounced someone eternally unforgivable. Do not invent their words or treat the author’s modern imaginative paraphrase as a recovered exchange.

## HT-02

**Jesus’s human life and the compatibility of humanity with divinity.**

- [Luke 2:52, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+2%3A52&version=NIV)
- [John 4:6, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+4%3A6&version=NIV)
- [Catechism of the Catholic Church, §§456–483, official Vatican edition](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1J.HTM)
- **Status:** biblical wording and the relevant Catechism paragraphs checked. The Catechism is a primary witness to Catholic teaching, not proof of every possible Christology.
- **Locator:** §§464, 467, 470–474. The human-mind and human-heart sentence in §470 quotes *Gaudium et spes* 22; identify that nested attribution.
- **Brief excerpt, §464:** “true God and true man”. The draft also uses short portions of §470.

Luke depicts growth; John depicts fatigue. Catholic teaching affirms genuine human nature while retaining divine personhood, uniqueness, and sinlessness. Paragraph 472 addresses knowledge exercised under historical conditions; §§473–474 immediately qualify that discussion through divine knowledge. Do not isolate §472 as an endorsement of arbitrary doctrinal error or collapse Catholic incarnation into the description “an inspired person.”

**Use:** the essay can invite closer attention to human speech and encounter without making denial of divinity a condition of that attention. It does not need to settle the relation of all Christian accounts of inspiration and incarnation.

## HT-03

**John’s unique Son, children of God, and the limits of the proposed reframe.**

- [John 3:16, Greek text](https://www.greekbible.com/john/3/16)
- [John 3:16, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A16&version=NIV)
- [John 1:12–14, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A12-14&version=NIV)
- [William D. Mounce, *monogenēs* entry and concordance](https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/monogenes)
- **Status:** Greek clause, named English excerpts, and dictionary entry checked. No full history of the only-begotten controversy or original-language patristic collation completed.
- **Dictionary excerpt:** “one and only, unique”. The entry also records older only-begotten/only-born glosses; a debate about generation should not be resolved from a root alone.

**Greek:** τὸν υἱὸν τὸν μονογενῆ.

**Romanization:** *ton huion ton monogenē*.

**Segmented literal gloss:** τὸν υἱὸν, the son; τὸν μονογενῆ, the only/unique one (the second article marks the attributive adjective).

**Closest English:** “the one and only Son” / “the only Son”; traditional “the only begotten Son.” These are alternatives within interpretation and translation history, not proof that all theological uses are identical.

**Contextual limit:** the referent in John 3:16 is Jesus. John 1:12 describes those who receive him and believe in his name as becoming children of God. It does not simply assert that everyone already has Jesus’s same unique status. Changing the referent to every person is not a defensible literal translation of *monogenēs*.

**Constructive use:** “each person loved as if an only child” can be offered openly as an image of wholehearted, non-expendable care. It should not be called the likely original meaning of John 3:16. The theological relationship between unique sonship and shared participation remains available for study without dominating the essay.

## HT-04

**The mother’s only child and care beyond the favored circle.**

- [*Discourse on Love*, Plum Village; Mettā Sutta, Sutta Nipāta 1.8](https://plumvillage.org/library/sutras/discourse-on-love)
- **Translation:** the page explicitly attributes the English to Thích Nhất Hạnh. Its commentary pointer is *Teachings on Love* (Parallax Press, 1998).
- [Matthew 5:44–45, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+5%3A44-45&version=NIV)
- **Status:** written translation and Gospel passage checked. No claim to have collated the Pali or read the whole commentary.
- **Quoted image:** the short mother/only-child phrase appears in the draft. It is a contiguous excerpt of the translation; the following instruction extends care to all beings.

**Important distinction:** the sutta instructs the practitioner to cultivate boundless care. It is not a statement about a creator God loving every being as an only child. Matthew joins enemy-love with the Father’s provision for evil and good. The essay brings these distinct teachings into conversation; it does not claim a shared historical source or erase their religious differences.

**Use:** move from incomparable care for one child toward the refusal to treat other lives as expendable. This is not the claim that care excuses harm, removes boundaries, or guarantees restoration of every relationship. Keep the comparison to two compact paragraphs in the main essay rather than opening a separate theology of sonship.

The publisher presents the book as TNH’s commentary on Linji and describes disruptive teaching methods alongside direct realization. Aaron’s comparison with a Zen teacher remains a useful framing question. Before attributing a particular saying, intention, or successful effect to Linji, obtain the text and its locator. ## HT-06

**Editorial analysis: words for the condition and limits of provocative teaching.**

This record is project analysis, not ancient terminology or a scholarly diagnosis.

- **Clinging to certainty** emphasizes how someone holds a view; this is the main descriptive phrase in the revised draft.
- **Closed-heartedness** can describe a relational effect, but must not become an accusation against doubt, trauma, or a protective boundary.
- **Dogmatism** names the doctrinal form of attachment. It is not a synonym for every doctrine or firm conviction.
- **Hardening of heart** is a biblical comparison whose application to disciples prevents an outsider-only reading. It is not lexically identical to blasphemy.
- **Fanaticism** is stronger and more accusatory. Do not use it merely because a person is zealous, disagrees, or holds a traditional interpretation.

A broad promise followed by an exception can be rhetorically forceful without being a formal contradiction. Koan-like attention does not prove that the exception is temporary, that Jesus’s speech was harmless, or that a future condition will change. Distinguish what a saying asserts, the response it may seek, and the response actually recorded. A teacher’s claimed compassionate intention cannot settle whether a provocation is truthful, proportionate, or coercive.

**Material decisions in this pass:** foreground narrative and shared humanity; make the Greek available without letting apparatus lead every movement; preserve the hardest finality passages once rather than repeatedly; retain accountable transformation; keep the only-child image as transparent constructive theology. These decisions implement Aaron’s direction while keeping historical and translation claims distinct.
