# Plum Village — lived insight, nonattachment to views, and human teachers

Initial checks: 2026-10-10. Use these sources as explicit comparative resources. The project should not claim that Buddhism lacks dogma or that Christianity lacks experiential inquiry.

## PV-07

**The Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings, official Plum Village text.**

- [Official text](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-14-mindfulness-trainings)
- **Version:** the page identifies the revision presented by TNH in February 2012.
- **Locators:** first training, “Openness”; second, “Non-Attachment to Views”; third, “Freedom of Thought”; sixth, “Taking Care of Anger.”
- **Status:** relevant written trainings checked.
- **Brief quotation:** “We are committed to seeing the Buddhist teachings as a guiding means”.

The text applies its warning about attachment to doctrines to Buddhist doctrines too. It joins continuing learning to lived practice, listening, and ethical conduct. This is a source for a self-critical spiritual tradition, not simply a tool with which to criticize another religion.

**Question:** How might this guide the essay’s own conduct—especially its willingness to encounter evidence against its preferred interpretation?

## PV-08

**Thích Nhất Hạnh, Old Path White Clouds: Walking in the Footsteps of the Buddha.**

- [Plum Village book page and attributed author excerpt](https://plumvillage.org/books/old-path-white-clouds-2)
- [Parallax Press catalog](https://www.parallax.org/product/old-path-white-clouds/)
- **Selected edition listed by Plum Village:** 1 May 1991, Parallax Press, ISBN 9780938077268. This is an edition listing, not a claim about the work’s earliest composition in Vietnamese.
- **Status:** attributed excerpt and catalog checked; full book not read here.
- **Brief author quotation:** “If the Buddha appears in this book as a man close to us”.

The surrounding author excerpt connects that proximity with recounting difficulties involving disciples and wider society. This is a direct lead for humanization through encounters and difficulties, rather than a generic claim that TNH made the Buddha “more human.”

**Limit:** the book is a literary retelling drawing on scriptural traditions, not an unmediated historical biography. Locate its introduction and source notes before making claims about how TNH adjudicates conflicting traditions.

## Questions to keep open

What is gained by humanizing a teacher, and what could be lost if “human” becomes only a modern ideal? How can lived practice inform understanding without treating any one practitioner’s experience as infallible? What theological claims about divinity are being proposed, rather than established by the historical record?
