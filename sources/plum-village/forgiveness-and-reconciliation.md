# Plum Village — forgiveness, reconciliation, and Beginning Anew

**Initial check:** 2026-10-10. These are comparative teaching resources, not evidence for the original meaning of Mark. Distinguish a practice of reconciliation from a demand that a harmed person reconcile or restore a former relationship.

## PV-01

**Plum Village, “Extended Practices,” section “Beginning Anew.”**

- [Official practice text](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/extended-practises)
- [Related “Begin Anew” page](https://plumvillage.org/articles/begin-anew)
- **Status:** written practice description checked. Brother Bao Tang’s embedded explanation was located; the recording was not reviewed.
- **Brief quotation from practice text:** “To begin anew is to look deeply and honestly at ourselves”.

The practice description includes appreciation, acknowledging regrets, expressing hurt, and asking for support with difficulties. It joins inward examination to speech and listening within relationships.

**Use in the project:** concrete practices can supply a comparison for repentance as changed conduct rather than remorse alone.

**Limit:** the practice text is not a universal safeguarding protocol, and its existence does not prove that reconciliation is possible or appropriate in every situation. Consent, safety, accountability, and power differences require separate attention.

## PV-02

**Sister Chan Duc / Sister Annabel Laity (True Virtue), “How To / Reconcile.”**

- [Official written excerpt](https://plumvillage.org/articles/reconcile)
- **Talk:** 7 January 2024, Lower Hamlet, Plum Village. **Web publication:** 26 March 2024.
- **Status:** written excerpt checked, not complete recording.
- **Brief quotation:** “We really need to know how to reconcile, how not to fight, but how to listen and speak”.

The excerpt brings Beginning Anew into dialogue with nonviolent communication. It emphasizes listening beyond one’s immediate perceptions and attending to suffering beneath speech.

**Use:** a teaching source for continuing discernment and altered perception in relationship.

**Limit:** do not infer that a person who cannot listen is simply unwilling or morally deficient; nor treat the excerpt as evidence that harmful conduct should be tolerated.
