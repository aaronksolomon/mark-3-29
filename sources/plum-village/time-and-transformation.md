## PV-04

**Sister Annabel Laity (Chan Duc, True Virtue), “The Three Dharma Seals.”**

- [The Mindfulness Bell / Parallax text](https://www.parallax.org/mindfulnessbell/article/the-three-dharma-seals/)
- **Talk:** 20 June 2016, Upper Hamlet, Plum Village, 21-Day Retreat: Vulture Peak Gathering.
- **Publication:** *The Mindfulness Bell*, February 2017, issue 74; confirm the print page if using a print citation.
- **Locator:** section “THE THREE TIMES INTER-ARE,” and the later paragraph beginning with the practice’s help in looking back over one’s life.
- **Status:** relevant written passages checked.
- **Brief quotation:** “The three times and the two truths inter-are.”

The teaching relates past, present, and future to one another rather than treating them as isolated compartments. Sister Annabel specifically considers past actions one regrets and the possibility of acting differently now as a way of healing one’s relationship with the past.

**Attribution:** this is a direct verified teaching by Sister Annabel. She presents it within Plum Village teaching. It should not be attributed verbatim to TNH unless a separate exact source is established.

**Interpretive boundary:** the project may use this to distinguish continued consequences from a fixed identity. It does not establish that the event never happened, that harmed people must accept a new narrative, or that every resistant condition will necessarily change.

## Proposed comparative paragraph — editorial, not a source quotation

A past action may remain present through its effects without fixing every possibility for the person who acted. A change of understanding does not erase the suffering of others. It can nevertheless change what the person does next. The comparative question is whether a warning against refusing release must also establish that refusal can never be transformed.

This paragraph is constructive theology informed by the comparison. It is not a translation of Mark and not a claim that Buddhist and Christian accounts of self, grace, time, or liberation are identical.
