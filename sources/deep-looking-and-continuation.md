# Deep looking, hearing, and continuation

**Scope and date:** focused source and editorial record, October 19, 2026, for the author-directed revision of v4 from `e959365c429c970c6a1d6da9abcf0b114b7f43d0`. This does not replace the existing language, Jewish, Plum Village, or interpretation-history dossiers. It gathers the sources that let the current revision ask about receptivity and transformation without making a fixed conclusion about the temporal force of Mark 3:29.

**Access distinction:** the written passages identified below were examined in the linked displays. No recording was independently transcribed, no full modern commentary volume was read, and no manuscript collation or specialist certification is claimed. Quotations generally appear in the essay rather than being duplicated here. The existing research records keep their original access limits.

## DC-01 — Mark 4 and the scriptural question of hearing

**Primary texts:** [Mark 4, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+4&version=NIV); [Isaiah 6, NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+6&version=NIV). The relevant passages were read in context, especially Mark 4:1–20, 21–29, 33–34, and Isaiah 6:8–13. See also [JC-10](jewish-context-and-cross-tradition-inquiry.md#jc-10).

The main text quotes Mark 4:12 continuously after explaining who asks about the parables and the contrast in verse 11. Mark's citation ends in forgiveness; Isaiah's phrasing concerns healing. Isaiah's commission includes grave judgment and devastation, not only a gentle observation about receptivity. Jesus's quotation, as Mark presents it, locates his teaching within an inherited scriptural conversation.

The essay asks what recalling these words might do within a compassionate address. That is different from claiming to have proved that the purpose clause means irony, that no divine concealment is possible, or that access to understanding depends only on a hearer's voluntary decision. Mark's disciples need explanation too. The light, seed-growth, and capacity-to-receive passages contribute to the inquiry; they do not mechanically cancel verse 12.

**Use:** an open exploration of how a teaching may disclose truth, reveal that it has not been understood, and become differently available through questioning and practice. The compassionate starting point is stated as a ground for inquiry, not evidence of a privately recovered intention.

## DC-02 — Deep looking and introduction of Thích Nhất Hạnh

**Order of Interbeing, The Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings:** [official text](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-14-mindfulness-trainings), First–Third Trainings. The Second explicitly joins insight with listening, deep looking, release of notions, and observation of life. The essay's short excerpts retain the distinction between learning and merely accumulating knowledge. Its questions about fear, loyalty, action, and effects are constructive applications.

**Biographical orientation:** [Plum Village biography](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/biography); [official overview](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh). These support introducing Thích Nhất Hạnh as a Vietnamese Buddhist monk, teacher, and peace activist; the Order was founded in 1966 and Plum Village developed in France from 1982. The overview gives 1926–2022. No current institutional office or status is inferred from older biographical language.

See [ND-01](nonattachment-and-nondual-reading.md#nd-01--openness-and-nonattachment-to-views). The newer author preference is to make deep looking concrete, rather than return repeatedly to formal-consistency safeguards. Evidence and ethical scrutiny remain part of the practice.

## DC-03 — The spoon and the tongue

**Dhammapada 63–65**, translated by Acharya Buddharakkhita, *The Dhammapada: The Buddha's Path of Wisdom* (Buddhist Publication Society, 1985), displayed at [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.05.budd.html), revised November 30, 2013. Named verses and neighboring text checked; Pali not independently collated.

Verse 64 compares proximity without understanding to a spoon unable to taste soup. Verse 65 contrasts the tongue's immediate tasting; verse 63 commends recognition of one's lack of understanding. These supply the attested comparison used in the essay.

**Attribution limit:** Aaron recalls Thích Nhất Hạnh using the image. A particular published passage or authenticated recording of his use was not located in this pass. Do not promote that recollection into an invented TNH quotation. The main text attributes the image to the Dhammapada. Nor should the simile turn temporary incomprehension into a permanent personal category.

## DC-05 — Temporal expressions as perspective, not a verdict

Retain [AS-04–AS-05](semitic-gospel-witnesses.md#as-04--peshitta-mark-32830-checked-wording), [TA-01–TA-02](time-age-and-eternity.md#ta-01--aiōn-and-english-eon), and the [Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon entry for ʿlm](https://cal.huc.edu/oneentry.php?cits=all&lemma=%28lm+N).

Greek *aiōn* and Aramaic *ʿālam* have different linguistic histories; their association is through overlapping use and translation. Mark's *eis ton aiōna* is singular; the Syriac *l-ʿālam* is not literally a plural phrase meaning all ages. The conventional denial of forgiveness remains emphatic. Root senses and idioms should be distinguished.

**New emphasis:** ask what we imagine when something is said to last. Does persistence require an unchanged object, a repeated condition, an enduring relation, or a continuation through changed effects? Those questions do not produce a different Greek clause. They also should not be dismissed as irrelevant merely because a dictionary gives a conventional translation. No general theory of ancient time is asserted.

## DC-06 — Continuity without an immutable bearer

**Plum Village, “Have We Understood the Buddha? (Part I),” October 25, 2024:** [written article](https://plumvillage.org/articles/have-we-understood-the-buddha-part-i), discussion of action, result, and the capacity to understand. Relevant written sections checked.

The article uses milk becoming butter to distinguish causal continuity from both immutable sameness and unrelated replacement. Its voice is not identified here as a verbatim TNH transcript. The essay's short expression appears there; the application to Mark is ours. It does not establish infinitely persistent, unchanged karmic results.

## DC-07 — Abrahamic comparisons: the past and a changed life

**Ezekiel 18:21–32, especially 22–23:** [NIV text](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezekiel+18%3A21-32&version=NIV). The chapter joins turning, right conduct, life, judgment, and a changed relation to previous wrongdoing. It does not say that harm was never suffered.

**John 12:23–26**, especially 24: [NIV text](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+12%3A24&version=NIV). The grain image belongs to Jesus's approaching death and the call to follow. Its comparison with continuation is not an equation of resurrection with Buddhist no-self. Existing John 5:24–29 and [TA-05](time-age-and-eternity.md#ta-05--eternal-life-as-present-and-future) remain.

**Qur'an 25:68–71**, especially 70, translated by Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*: [text and context](https://quran.com/25/70). Verse 70 concerns repentance, faith, good action, and divine mercy transforming evil deeds. The preceding warning is severe. The essay does not decide the historical range of interpretations of this transformation or imply that all consequences are abolished. No full tafsir survey or independent Arabic collation was undertaken.

These witnesses allow a question across traditions: how may the past remain real without being the only determinant of the person's present relation to truth, God, or others? They retain their distinct religious commitments; divine pardon, karmic result, changed conduct, and restored trust are not equivalent terms.

## DC-08 — Islamic counsel concerning speech and mercy

**Qur'an 16:125–128**, translated by Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*: [text](https://quran.com/16/125). Named verse and context checked. The main text quotes its counsel concerning wise address and manner of debate. The final clause assigns fullest knowledge of guidance to God. The following verses address retaliation, patience, and restraint, so this is not a blanket prohibition of moral judgment or a declaration of equivalence among religions.

**Qur'an 39:53–54:** [text](https://quran.com/39/53-54), same translation. Checked and retained as a reserve comparison rather than another main-text paragraph. Expansive mercy stands beside a call to turn before punishment arrives. Do not detach the assurance from the adjacent call.

The Islamic contribution is a later comparative voice, not a replacement for Jesus's Jewish historical setting. Two narrowly related main-text uses—speech and transformed deeds—are sufficient for this pass.
