# Interpretation history — primary texts and their limits

Selected passages checked on 2026-10-10 in the linked English editions. These notes are a reception map, not an exhaustive history. No original-language patristic critical edition has yet been collated. The ancient and medieval authors are not interchangeable, and an isolated hopeful sentence must retain its context.

## R-01

**Didache, chapter 11.**

- [English text, New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0714.htm)
- **Status:** relevant chapter checked. Exact composition date and literary relation to the Gospels remain research questions.
- **Brief quotation:** “for every sin shall be forgiven, but this sin shall not be forgiven”.

The warning is applied to testing or judging a prophet speaking in the Spirit. The same chapter then distinguishes genuine and false prophets by conduct. This is an early adaptation of the saying to community discernment, not direct evidence of Jesus’s original occasion.

**Question:** How does the text protect perceived inspiration while preserving tests of conduct? Could the warning also be used to insulate a claimant from criticism?

## R-02

**John Chrysostom, Homily 41 on Matthew, especially §5.**

- [English translation, New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/200141.htm)
- **Edition note:** the site identifies George Prevost’s translation, revised by M. B. Riddle, in NPNF, first series, vol. 10 (1888).
- **Status:** relevant argument and surrounding polemic checked, not Greek critical text.
- **Brief quotation:** “For even this was forgiven upon repentance.”

Chrysostom says some who made the accusation later believed and received pardon. This is Chrysostom’s retrospective claim, not independently verified biographies of the scribes in Mark. He also stresses culpability and punishment in this life and the next. The surrounding passage contains anti-Jewish polemic, including the use of collective historical suffering in the argument.

**Use:** early Greek-speaking interpretation did not simply make any utterance an automatic barrier to later repentance.

**Limit:** do not present the homily as an uncomplicated affirmation of universal restoration or harmless theological discussion. Its severe and harmful reception potential belongs in the research record.

## R-03

**Augustine, Sermon 71 (LXXI); numbered Sermon 21 in this English collection.**

- [English text, New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/160321.htm)
- **Locator:** discussion of impenitence, especially §§20–22.
- **Status:** selected passages checked in English; original Latin and edition history to verify.
- **Brief quotation:** “For we are not to despair of any”.

Augustine locates the finality in impenitence maintained through the end of life and warns against pronouncing it upon a living person. His ecclesial account has conditions of unity and repentance; it is not a general doctrine that every possible refusal is eventually healed.

**Question:** How much does identifying the sin as final impenitence clarify Mark, and how much does it add from a larger theology of death and repentance?

## R-04

**Thomas Aquinas, Summa theologiae, II–II, question 14, article 3.**

- [English text, New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3014.htm)
- **Locator:** “Whether the sin against the Holy Ghost can be forgiven?”; answer and replies, not merely the opening objections.
- **Status:** relevant article checked in English; Latin critical text not collated.
- **Brief quotation:** “This does not, however, close the way of forgiveness and healing to an all-powerful and merciful God”.

Aquinas distinguishes Augustine’s final-impenitence account from another sense in which the sin rejects the things through which pardon ordinarily comes. In the latter sense, resistance to remedy does not limit extraordinary divine healing. His discussion is not a guarantee of universal postmortem restoration.

**Use:** a condition opposing its cure and a condition beyond every possible cure are not necessarily identical.

## R-05

**John Calvin, Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists, Matthew, Mark, and Luke; discussion of Matthew 12:31–32 and parallels.**

- [CCEL English text](https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom32/calcom32.ii.xiii.html)
- **Status:** relevant discussion checked; exact print translation details to confirm for final bibliography.
- **Brief quotation:** “they never have any desire towards repentance”.

Calvin distinguishes ignorance, exemplified by Paul, from malicious rejection of recognized divine truth. On his reading the completed sin signals irreversible hardening: the person will not subsequently desire repentance. That makes a repentant applicant being rejected unnecessary, but makes irreversibility a major interpretive premise.

**Question:** Is the opponents’ inward recognition established by Mark or supplied by the theological interpretation? Do not present Calvin’s account as the position of every Protestant tradition.

## R-06

**George MacDonald, Unspoken Sermons. Two distinct sermons are relevant.**

- [Combined text, Project Gutenberg eBook 9057](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/9057/pg9057.html)
- **Status:** relevant passages checked in the combined English text.
- **Locator 1:** *It Shall Not Be Forgiven*.
- **Brief quotation:** “But I do not believe that it is a fixed, a final condition.”
- **Locator 2:** *Light*; the paragraph that considers resistance after a person has rejected truth.
- **Second brief phrase:** “a whole age or era”.

In the first sermon MacDonald resists treating spiritual closure as final. In *Light* he considers an age of unforgiven sin within a remedial account in which repentance and coming to the light remain possible. He does not remove the seriousness or pain of judgment.

**Locator correction:** earlier conversation loosely associated the age-long passage with *It Shall Not Be Forgiven*. The combined volume places that discussion in *Light*. Cite the right sermon.

**Limit:** this is a constructive theological reading, not a demonstration that Mark’s idiom literally means “until the age ends.”

## R-07

**Catechism of the Catholic Church, §1864.**

- [Official Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_one/chapter_one/article_8/iv_the_gravity_of_sin_mortal_and_venial_sin.html)
- **Status:** paragraph checked.
- **Brief quotation:** “There are no limits to the mercy of God”.

The paragraph understands the problem as deliberate refusal of mercy through rejecting repentance; such hardening can lead to final impenitence and loss. Its initial affirmation must not be separated from that qualification.

**Question:** Does this explain the condition of non-forgiveness, its eventual irreversibility, or both? What comes from Mark and what comes from the wider doctrine?

## R-08

**John Paul II, Dominum et vivificantem, §46 (18 May 1986).**

- [Official Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_18051986_dominum-et-vivificantem.html)
- **Status:** relevant section checked. No quotation is needed to preserve the distinction below.

The encyclical connects non-forgiveness with radical rejection of conversion and describes the sources of redemption as remaining open. This supplies a relational account of self-exclusion, not evidence that the Greek explicitly contains a “while unrepentant” clause.

**Question:** How does the continuing availability of redemption relate to final impenitence? A later section’s theological synthesis should not be substituted for lexical analysis.

## R-09

**Second Vatican Council, Dei verbum, §19 (18 November 1965).**

- [Official Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html)
- **Status:** relevant paragraph checked.
- **Brief quotation:** “selecting some things from the many which had been handed on”.

The constitution recognizes evangelists’ selection, synthesis, and explanation in light of their churches’ circumstances, while also affirming Gospel truth. It is useful precisely because acknowledgment of interpretation is not confined to a rejection of traditional Christian authority.

**Limit:** the document does not endorse the essay’s every possible claim about fallibility. Human literary activity and doctrinal conclusions must remain distinct.

## R-10

**Further ancient reception — acquisition stage.**

Athanasius, *Fourth Letter to Serapion*, especially 4.17, is a priority lead from the bibliography of [S-01](modern-scholarship.md#s-01). Obtain a reliable edition and the full argument before summarizing. Origen and other early discussions of postmortem correction may be relevant, but no specific interpretation of Mark is attributed to them here without a checked passage.

## Reception questions to carry into the draft

The interpreters move the decisive boundary in different ways: historical knowledge, deliberate resistance, refusal of repentance, death, reprobation, or restorative judgment beyond present refusal. Record what each adds and the cost of that addition. The existence of disagreement does not make all interpretations equally probable; it shows why a translation cannot settle the entire theology.
