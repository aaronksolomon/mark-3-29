## Priority C — ancient reception in fuller context

- **Athanasius, Fourth Letter to Serapion, especially 4.17.** The 2026 bibliography points to *Athanasius Werke* I/1,4, ed. K. Savvidis (2010), pp. 583–584. Confirm locator and read surrounding argument.
- **Chrysostom, Homily 41 on Matthew.** English passage checked; obtain Greek and investigate the relation between repentance, knowledge, punishment, and anti-Jewish polemic.
- **Augustine, Sermon 71.** English selected passages checked; verify a critical Latin edition. Do not copy potentially inconsistent edition metadata from a secondary bibliography without checking.
- **Aquinas, II–II q.14.** English article checked; compare Latin and article 1’s taxonomy before isolating article 3’s hopeful clause.
- **Didache 11.** Read a modern critical edition and commentary on prophecy/discernment, including composition-date uncertainties.
- **Gospel of Thomas 44; Hebrews 6:4–6; 1 John 5:16.** Adjacent hard-limit texts noted by Kręcidło. They complicate the project; do not omit them simply because they are severe.
- **Origen and later restoration traditions.** Locate exact primary discussions. Do not attribute Mark interpretations to an author solely because they held views about correction or restoration elsewhere.
- **George MacDonald, *It Shall Not Be Forgiven* and *Light*.** Separate sermon locators already established in [R-06](interpretation-history.md#r-06); read both in full.

## Priority D — Jewish scripture and semantic corpus

Build a small table with date range, language, passage, lexical form, metaphor, social/ritual context, and interpretive uncertainty. Start with Exodus 34:6–7; Numbers 14:18–23 and 15:27–31; Leviticus 25:10; Psalms of pardon; Isaiah 5:20; Septuagint counterparts. Add Second Temple and rabbinic materials only with care about date and diversity.

Questions: Where is pardon compatible with continuing consequences? Where are debts, burdens, impurity, and wrongdoing distinct? How do deliberate and inadvertent acts differ? What would falsify the proposed semantic progression?

## Priority E — John, humanity, and living interpretation

- David F. Ford, *The Gospel of John: A Theological Commentary*, and the [Nicodemus article lead](modern-scholarship.md#s-07).
- A critical John commentary on 3:21; 7:17; 8:31–36; 9:39–41; 13:17; 15:1–17; 16:12–15.
- Study classical affirmations of Jesus’s full humanity and sinlessness alongside non-inerrant and constructive Christologies. Do not equate human limitation automatically with moral error, or assume the project’s theology is already classical doctrine.

## Simple record for the next acquired work

Record source ID, full edition, access date, what was actually read, short exact quotation with page or timestamp, paraphrase, relevance, strongest counterevidence, and next question. Do not mark “read” when only metadata was available.
