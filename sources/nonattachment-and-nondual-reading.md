# Nonattachment, nondual reading, and the limits of formulations

**Scope:** a focused addition following Aaron’s revision `6801a0b`. Sources checked 2026-10-10. These notes support the dogmatism discussion, the human transmission section, and the time/practice section. They are not a survey of Buddhist logic or a claim that the Gospels secretly teach one Buddhist system.

**Working distinction:** rigidity protects a judgment from correction; dogmatism makes that protection a rule of belief, sometimes an institutional demand. These are the essay’s working definitions, not recovered Greek meanings. Christian and Buddhist sources participate in the inquiry directly. Attribution distinguishes what a source says from what we propose to learn through it.

## ND-01 — Openness and nonattachment to views

**Order of Interbeing / Plum Village, The Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings**, revision presented by Thích Nhất Hạnh in February 2012.

- [Official text](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-14-mindfulness-trainings)
- **Locators:** First Training, “Openness”; Second, “Non-Attachment to Views.”
- **Status:** written text checked. Complements [PV-07](plum-village/living-teaching-and-humanity.md#pv-07), rather than replacing that record.
- **Brief quotation:** “transform dogmatism and violence in ourselves and the world.”

The First Training includes Buddhist doctrines within its warning against attachment; the Second connects revisability with listening and lived inquiry. Use these as practices that also question our preferred Buddhist or Christian interpretation, not as proof that either community is free of dogmatism. The trainings are commitments, not descriptions of all practitioners’ actual behavior.

## ND-02 — Historical and ultimate; neither monism nor separation

**Thích Nhất Hạnh, interviewed by Melvin McLeod, “This Is the Buddha’s Love,” Shambhala Sun, March 2006; republished by Plum Village.**

- [Interview text](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/shambhala-sun-march-2006-exclusive-interview)
- **Locators:** questions beginning “I very much like the way you describe…” and “Some people I have spoken to seem to interpret…”; the following discussion of the Buddha’s humanity.
- **Status:** relevant interview passages checked, not an audio transcription independently verified here.
- **Brief quotation:** “interbeing does not mean that everything is one or that everything is different.”

The wave/water example relates historical and ultimate dimensions without separating them into two worlds. TNH also rejects making interbeing into an assertion that everything is simply one. This supports examination of how descriptions are held; it does not establish a universal Buddhist formal logic. The essay’s application to responsibility after transformation is its own argument.

## ND-03 — The times and the truths

**Plum Village editors and community teachers, “The Four Dharma Seals of Plum Village,” 5 November 2022.**

- [Official article](https://plumvillage.org/articles/the-four-dharma-seals-of-plum-village)

- **Status:** written editorial text checked; linked talks not listened to in this pass.
- **Brief quotation:** “The times and the truths inter-are.”

The article identifies the three times and specifies that “truths” includes conventional/ultimate truth and the Four Noble Truths. It attributes teaching to Sister Chân Đức and Brother Pháp Ứng rather than presenting every editorial sentence as TNH’s speech. See also [PV-04](plum-village/time-and-transformation.md#pv-04), Sister Annabel Laity’s account of present practice and past regret.

**Use:** connect ethical continuation with transformation. **Limit:** these sources do not alter the semantics of Mark’s age-language, establish a physics of time, or make past suffering unreal.

## ND-04 — A koan involving the reader

**Thích Nhất Hạnh, “Bat Nha: a Koan,” official Plum Village letter.**

- [Letter](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/letters/bat-nha-a-koan)
- **Locators:** opening explanation of koan practice; paragraphs beginning “A koan cannot…” and “If you think Bat Nha…”; the subsequent imagined first-person viewpoints.
- **Status:** relevant written passages checked. The first-person roles are TNH’s literary devices, not separately verified witness statements.
- **Brief quotation:** “Bat Nha truly becomes a koan only when you understand it as your own problem”.

The letter makes a crisis involving persecution an inquiry in which the practitioner is implicated, rather than an external puzzle. It distinguishes contemplative realization from intellectual resolution. Our proposed koan-like reading of Mark concerns this involvement of the reader, not historical identity between Gospel parables and Zen koans. Do not suggest that staying indefinitely distressed is itself the goal, or use the analogy to silence factual or ethical scrutiny.

## ND-05 — A healing outside the disciples’ group

**Mark 9:23–24,38–41**, with Matthew 12:30 as a comparison.

- [Mark 9, NABRE text hosted by USCCB](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/mark/9)
- [Matthew 12, text and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/12)
- **Status:** Mark’s passages checked; Matthew’s contrasting saying retained as a comparison to examine in its own context.
- **Brief quotation from Mark 9:38:** “because he does not follow us.”

The objection is to the exorcist’s relation to the disciples’ group, not simply absence of relation to Jesus: he acts in Jesus’s name. Jesus rejects their prohibition. Earlier, the father’s confession holds trust and unbelief together without preventing the healing story from continuing.

**Use:** a particularly close comparison with Mark 3, since both concern responses to exorcism. **Limit:** neither episode proves that every claimed healing is genuine or every religious belief equally sound. Group nonmembership and metaphysical nonduality are different issues.

## ND-06 — The neighbor and worship beyond rival locations

**Luke 10:25–37 and John 4:9–26,39–42.**

- [Luke 10](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/10)
- [John 4](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/4)
- **Status:** relevant biblical passages checked in the USCCB-hosted NABRE. The argument uses the text, not every generalization in the accompanying notes.
- **Brief quotation, Luke 10:37:** “Go and do likewise.”

Luke’s legal expert answers with the commandments to love God and neighbor, and Jesus affirms him before telling the Samaritan story. Mercy across a boundary is therefore presented within a Jewish scriptural conversation, not as Christianity’s correction of an unloving Judaism.

John’s Samaritan interlocutor calls Jacob an ancestor. The encounter crosses a real religious division within an intertwined Israelite inheritance. In 4:21–24 Jesus speaks beyond the rivalry of worship sites while also making a particular claim about salvation’s Jewish origin. Later the Samaritans describe hearing for themselves. Do not erase either boundary-crossing or the text’s commitments.

**Use:** test the assumption that the good must belong to our group. **Limit:** moral exemplarity, interreligious openness, and nondual ontology should not be conflated. “Truth is not a tradition’s possession” is the essay’s constructive position, not a verse quoted from these passages.

## ND-07 — Unity without erasing relation

**John 17:20–23**, within Jesus’s prayer for present and future believers.

- [John 17, NABRE and notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/17)
- [Greek 17:21](https://www.greekbible.com/john/17/21)
- **Status:** Greek clause and surrounding English passage checked. No manuscript apparatus examined in this pass.

**Greek:** ἵνα πάντες ἓν ὦσιν.

**Romanization:** *hina pantes hen ōsin*.

**Segmented gloss:** ἵνα, that/so that; πάντες, all; ἓν, one; ὦσιν, may be.

**Study rendering:** “that they may all be one.” **Alternative:** “so that all of them may be one.”

The following clauses describe the Father in Jesus, Jesus in the Father, and believers in them. Unity is expressed through relations and mutual indwelling. A nondual reading can explore this as communion without isolated selves; traditional readings can understand ecclesial and participatory unity without identifying God and creatures. The text invites the comparison but does not establish either metaphysical identity with Buddhism or the historical Jesus’s precise interior experience. John’s particular revelation claims remain part of the passage.

## ND-08 — Logic and the choice of a description

**Status:** project analysis, not a historical source or a grammatical paraphrase of Mark.

Separate four questions: is an inference valid; are its premises true; is its description adequate to the situation; and has the truth it concerns been realized in life? None answers all the others.

For example, let `R(p,t)` mean that a person repudiates mercy at a time, and `A(p,t)` that the person receives it. Even if we propose `R(p,t) -> not A(p,t)`, this does not entail `R(p,t')` or `not A(p,t')` for every later time `t'`. Conversely, ceasing to satisfy `R` does not, by this implication alone, guarantee `A`. Both stronger conclusions need additional premises. This illustrates how reasoning can expose an unwarranted inference; it does not prove that Mark intends a merely temporary condition. His temporal language is exactly what the interpretation must still examine.

Do not attribute permanence, exclusive social categories, or all dogmatism to modern logic. Do not describe Buddhist logic as one agreed calculus that simply permits contradictions. The focused question is whether a selected formulation is being treated as an exhaustive account of reality. “Not one, not two” need not become a numerical claim or a waiver of consistency.
