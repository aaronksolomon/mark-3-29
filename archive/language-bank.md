# Language bank — selected formulations, not settled conclusions

Provenance is marked for each group. Formatting is normalized. These are writing materials from the supplied conversation, not quotations from scholarly or religious authorities. Where a phrase appears in both assistant prose and Aaron’s later revision, that shared history is noted.

## Personal responsibility

**Assistant draft language, later themes retained by Aaron:**

> Forgiveness does not mean that nothing happened. Forgiveness does not erase consequences. Forgiveness does not make reconciliation automatic, nor does it remove the need for truth, accountability, restitution, repentance, and change. Perhaps forgiveness means that even after real harm, we refuse to say that the harm is the final truth about a human being.

> Understanding the conditions that gave rise to an action is not the same as excusing the action.

> The person who has caused harm is not necessarily finished becoming. And the harm is still real.

**Aaron’s own revision:**

> I have come to recognize that my intentions do not determine the consequences of my actions. I failed to see something important about myself, about the power and responsibility that I held, and the situation in which I found myself.

> I cannot make another person’s experience conform to my understanding or longing.

**Caution:** do not add particulars or turn assistant first-person elaboration into biographical evidence. Accountability is not a claim to reconciliation.

## Transmission and humanity

**Assistant formulation subsequently retained by Aaron:**

> Perhaps revelation has always traveled through human beings rather than around them.

**Assistant question from the humanization stages:**

> What would it mean to trust Jesus enough to allow his humanity to be real?

**Assistant formulation:**

> The evangelists are interpreters as well as transmitters.

**Caution:** human participation does not establish a particular historical mistake. “Interpretation” is not a synonym for fabrication; “human” is not a proof of moral error. A fuller history must also allow preservation, correction, and faithful understanding.

## Practice and inquiry

**Aaron’s revision:**

> The Gospel looks like something we understand by walking.

**Shared formulation, present in assistant drafts and Aaron’s revision:**

> We do not have to choose between reverence and inquiry.
>
> Perhaps deep reverence requires deep inquiry.

**Assistant formulation:**

> Some truths may become visible only from inside the life they ask us to live.

**Caution:** none of these claims means experience is infallible, tradition is dispensable, or John’s Gospel offers a simple procedure for proving any preferred interpretation.

## Refusal, release, and transformation

**Aaron’s conceptual proposal:**

> If you refuse to see, you will not see.

**Assistant interpretive development:**

> The blindness has no cure while the cure itself is named the enemy.

**Assistant refinement:**

> When the refusal is interrupted—through willingness, encounter, suffering, grace, or the working of the Spirit—the condition itself begins to change.

**Caution:** the “while” is an interpretive qualification absent from Mark’s explicit wording. Not every inability to see is refusal. Insight need not result from choice alone. Mark’s “never” and Matthew’s two-age language remain contrary evidence to face.

## Closing language that resonated in the thread

**Assistant closing, selected passage:**

> We do not stand outside the long human story of misunderstanding and awakening. We belong to it. We walk with the disciples who did not understand, with the religious teachers who feared they were being deceived, with Paul who changed his mind and his life, with the evangelists who struggled to preserve what they had received, and with generations who have tried to understand what Jesus was pointing toward.
>
> We too see only partially. Yet we can continue walking.

**Caution:** the teachers’ fear is a humane imaginative possibility, not an established historical motive. In scholarly prose it needs qualification.

**Aaron’s revised closing:**

> To remain in the teachings on love, and to see what becomes true in our lives, to let the Gospel live in us and our communities.

## Language retained for scrutiny rather than reuse

- “All will be forgiven” as a free-standing guarantee about every person: distinguish sins from individuals and read the exception.
- “The consequences of seeing good as evil are extremely severe” as a translation of Mark 3:29: a possible interpretation, not the Greek wording.
- “Perhaps grace means that even the consequences can become teachers”: growth does not justify harm or make suffering necessary.
- “The Word continues to become flesh”: a constructive devotional metaphor, not a conclusion of lexical analysis.
- “Relational vocabulary increasingly acquires legal associations”: needs a documented semantic history and ancient counterexamples.

These tensions are part of the project’s history. See [claim-audit.md](../notes/claim-audit.md) before promoting them.
