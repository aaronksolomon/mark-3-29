## 1. Overall finding and scope

Keep the six-section structure. The essay has a coherent movement: a severe saying is heard within a human encounter; close reading preserves its difficulty; the history of interpretation exposes consequential choices; comparative teachings bring the inquiry into perception and practice; the conclusion returns to the humanity of those involved and the responsibility of the person reading.

The principal polishing opportunity is not wholesale compression. It is removing sentences that announce, qualify, or summarize a movement the adjacent sentences already make. The developed examples, connecting language, and deliberate returns to Mark are the essay's means of thinking, not padding.

Three distinctions should guide the next pass:

- **A return is not automatically a repetition.** Compassion in Jesus's address, responsibility in interpretation, and care in our own speech are related but distinct developments. Keep the returns that change the question.
- **A qualification needs a home, not constant repetition.** Keep the consequential limits beside the inference they qualify and in the notes. Do not repeatedly explain that comparisons are not identities or that transformation does not erase history.
- **Stronger prose need not make stronger historical claims.** A direct sentence can still describe a proposed hearing. Do not strengthen the essay by pretending to know Jesus's unrecorded intention, the scribes' inner motives, or the future of those addressed.

The Islamophobia passage is the one place where substantive strengthening is warranted. The present body establishes that hostility exists, but not the more consequential point: public stereotypes and discriminatory treatment can make a religious identity stand in for a person before an encounter has begun. The replacement in section 4 below gives that point more force while keeping the evidence in a note.

**Scope of checking:** the prose and publication apparatus were read at the pinned revision; the current Islamophobia references and the proposed national/U.S. and European evidence were checked in primary institutional publications. This is not a new specialist certification of Greek, Syriac, Hebrew, or Arabic, a complete re-audit of every historical quotation, or a PDF-layout review. The Mascaró adaptation is now recorded as author-verified on October 28; the earlier statement that author verification was outstanding should not be revived.

## 2. What to preserve

Do not remove or substantially rebuild the following in a tightening pass:

- The complete italicized NIV opening and the distinction between what the warning says, what its utterance may seek, and what happens to its hearers.
- The narrative movement through Mark 1–3, including the shared Jewish inheritance, the reversal between accusations concerning forgiveness and healing, and the possible effect on the wider circle.
- The complete Greek and Syriac presentations. They are an expressly chosen feature, not an accidental digression. Tighten the framing around them instead.
- The open treatment of Mark 4, especially **“Parabolic teaching can both reveal and expose an inability to hear.”** Do not replace its questions with a settled psychological explanation of concealment.
- The distinction between transformation and forgiveness; Paul's role as a test of fixed descriptions, not proof that he committed the exact act in Mark.
- The sentence **“His divinity need not be protected by making that life less human.”**
- The practical protection against imposed forgiveness, unsafe reconciliation, and the equation of disagreement with spiritual refusal.
- **“A warning does not enter an empty space.”** The surrounding speech section gives the warning's compassionate reading an ethical test, rather than merely asserting loving intention.
- The last question, including **“even ourselves.”** The conclusion should end in an invitation, not a newly decisive claim about final destiny.

## 4. Stronger Islamophobia passage

### Diagnosis

The current paragraph gives an institutional warning and a state-level example of recorded crime. Both sources are real and relevant, but the prose underuses the available evidence and ends with an abstract instruction to “notice.” The pasted search-engine overview should be removed, not promoted into a source.

The strongest improvement is to connect three things: documented prejudice and discrimination; the way collective suspicion substitutes a category for a person; and the practical responsibility to oppose exclusion. This should replace the current Islamophobia paragraph **and the following “We can question a doctrine” paragraph**, not be added on top of them.

### I01 — Preferred replacement body

> Anti-Muslim prejudice is not confined to isolated acts of hostility. U.S. polling records increased endorsement of negative stereotypes, while European research documents rising discrimination reported by Muslims in employment and housing. Discrimination and religious bullying also remain widespread in Muslim Americans' accounts of their lives. These are restrictions on safety, opportunity, and belonging, not simply disagreements about belief. [Retain the source call n71 here.]
>
> A religious identity can become a verdict delivered before a person speaks. When Muslims are treated as collectively suspect, we cease to ask what this person has said or done; the category supplies the answer. We can scrutinize a doctrine or oppose violence without making every member of a faith answerable for it. The responsibility extends beyond listening: it includes challenging discriminatory treatment, protecting the freedom to worship, and taking seriously the testimony of those affected. Muslims need not persuade us of their beliefs to deserve equal dignity. What happens to our professed openness when a person from a tradition we fear asks to be heard?

**Why this is stronger:** it names a pattern rather than merely the existence of incidents; keeps the human consequences visible; distinguishes scrutiny of beliefs from collective blame; and makes listening part of practical responsibility rather than the whole remedy. The ethical second paragraph is this essay's application, not a causal finding claimed for the surveys. It does not identify Islamophobia with Mark's named sin or turn Muslims into an illustration whose only purpose is someone else's spiritual growth.

The final question leads naturally into “Working with dogmatism begins with noticing our own attachment.” Keep the existing warning that a contrary interpretation is not itself evidence of a closed heart.

### I02 — Proposed replacement for n71

The following is a ready-to-adapt source note. Keep the statistics here rather than packing the main prose with figures.

> Institute for Social Policy and Understanding, *American Muslim Poll 2025*, “Islamophobia Index”: the general-public score rose from 25 in 2022 to 33 in 2025 on a 0–100 scale of endorsement of five anti-Muslim stereotypes. These are index points, not percentages or crime counts. Its May 2026 correction affected three subgroup scores, not this general-public comparison. The report's discrimination/bullying findings separately record 63% of Muslim respondents reporting religious discrimination in the prior year and 47% of Muslim respondents with K–12 children reporting religious bullying of a child; these measures remained broadly stable, not newly rising. European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights, *Being Muslim in the EU* (2024), reporting its 2022 survey: 47% reported racial discrimination in the preceding five years, compared with 39% in the 2016 survey, with increases in employment and housing. The selected immigrant-origin populations across 13 EU countries do not represent every Muslim in Europe. FRA's category includes religion, skin colour, and ethnic or immigrant background. These sources measure different phenomena and periods; they are not a combined trend or a count of hate crimes.

Use the primary links in the source record below. Exact final wording may be divided between the published note and a research dossier if the note becomes unwieldy, but do not remove the distinction between a rising attitudes index and stable reported U.S. discrimination/bullying.

### I03 — Important corrections to the pasted overview

1. **The U.S. attitudes trend is supported; the implied across-the-board increase is not.** ISPU's overview expressly describes the discrimination and bullying measures as broadly stable across previous polls. High prevalence is consequential without describing it as a rise. [S2]
2. **The school figure concerns respondents/families with K–12 children, not a percentage of all Muslim children.** Preserve the denominator. [S2]
3. **The European dates matter.** A report published in 2024 uses a 2022 survey and compares selected measures with 2016. Do not relabel this as a 2026 survey or treat it as evidence of changes after October 2023. [S3]
4. **Do not convert all measures into “hate crimes.”** Public attitudes, self-reported discrimination, reported bullying, complaints, and police-recorded crimes are not interchangeable.
5. **Avoid “ever increasing,” “unprecedented,” or an unqualified U.S./European crime surge.** The available series fluctuate and measure different things. A stronger ethical paragraph does not require maximal statistical language.

### I04 — What to do with the older sources

The current OSCE statement remains useful as documentation of threats, assaults, and attacks on places of worship, but it is not a national prevalence survey. The California release supports the existing bounded state-level statement; it does not establish a U.S.-wide rate. Keep them in the research dossier, or as supplementary references, rather than making them carry the entire comparative claim. [S5–S6]

For an additional current U.S. cross-check, Pew's September 3, 2026 report records substantial suspicion of Muslim Americans in a May 2026 survey: 43% described them as less patriotic than other Americans, and 42% described their impact on the country as negative. This independently supports the proposed focus on collective suspicion. It is corroboration, not another necessary paragraph or evidence of a trend in itself. [S4]

Do not expand the essay into electoral analysis, a list of perpetrators, or a catalogue of every religion's experience of persecution. The proposed two paragraphs are sufficient for the role this topic plays.

## 6. Repeated constructions: an editing rule, not a global replacement list

The prose repeatedly uses “gives … a form,” “brings … into view,” “offers a way,” “the question is,” and negative contrasts such as “not simply,” “not only,” and “need not.” None is objectionable in itself. The difficulty arises when an abstract introduction is followed by a concrete example and then a sentence restating the introduction.

For each such paragraph, ask which of those three parts is actually needed. Often the example plus one clear interpretation is enough. Elsewhere a transition is necessary to keep the reader oriented. Do not use a thesaurus to replace “hearing,” “care,” or “understanding” merely because they recur: these are governing terms whose recurrence gives the essay continuity.

Specific applications:

- Keep the difference between compassion and reassurance, but do not reannounce it after every source quotation.
- Let an example of harmful judgment do some work before naming dogmatism again.
- Prefer a concrete object—an accusation, a person, an action, a passage—to an ambiguous “this,” “it,” “its force,” or “the relation.”
- Keep deliberate questions that open a difficulty. Remove the broad preliminary question when the next sentence asks the precise one.
- Split long sentences where two different tasks meet; do not shorten every sentence to the same cadence.
- Use the contemporary case to make the ethical responsibility sharper, not to expand the essay's subject.
