## Overall reading

The essay is now in the stage of final shaping, not reconstruction. Its movement holds: a human encounter leads into close attention to the words, the responsibility of interpreters, and the question of how an understanding becomes a way of living. The preferred hearing is clear without pretending to settle what the text leaves difficult.

Its central achievement is a compassionate reading that remains answerable to the warning’s severity. It distinguishes what the saying declares, what speaking it may seek to do, and what eventually happens to those addressed. It also submits its own interpretation to the openness it asks of others. “That reading deserves an answer, not a diagnosis of closed-mindedness” is a particularly important sentence.

The remaining weakness is mostly overexplanation. At several points an insight is stated, qualified, and then stated or qualified again nearby. The strongest next pass would remove those echoes while preserving the connecting language through which the thought unfolds. There is no word-count target here, and a shorter sentence is not automatically a better one.

## What should remain

- The six-section arc and the opening’s gradual movement from the saying into the human encounter.
- The positive Jewish grounding and continuing Jewish voices. They do substantive work, not merely corrective work.
- The complete print-visible Greek study and the difficult evidence in Matthew 12 and Mark 4. Do not trim away what challenges the preferred hearing.
- The distinction among assertion, possible purpose, and outcome. Preserve one clear qualification where an inference is made rather than accumulating qualifications everywhere.
- The developed only-child comparison, the positive humanity/divinity account, and “This is not a choice between logic and insight.”
- Paul’s distinct witnesses, the difference between transformation and absolution, and the protections for injured people and honest disagreement.
- “A warning does not enter an empty space,” the abiding/doing-truth passage, and the conclusion’s fuller humanity paragraph.
- “Anemic in practice” and the final question, unchanged.

A return is not necessarily repetition. The warning’s movement from Jesus’s speech to later interpretation and then to our conduct is the essay’s structure. The proposals below chiefly target sentences doing the same work beside one another, not every recurrence of forgiveness, recognition, responsibility, or care.

## How to use the proposals

**Recommend** identifies a local change with a clear editorial gain. **Optional** identifies a preference to test aloud or in context, not a correction that must be accepted. The most useful first group is P02, P05, P07, P09, P11, P13–P15. Consider the optional concluding trim only after the body has been tightened.

Each **Original** is an exact excerpt from the fixed source above. Each **Proposed** supplies replacement wording, a specified deletion, or a paragraph break. Line links point to that fixed revision, not to whatever the working draft later becomes. Where an excerpt is only part of a paragraph, the surrounding text stays unchanged. Note calls such as [^n49] are the essay’s existing Markdown note identifiers; they are retained literally in the proposals and refer to the essay’s notes.

No wholesale rewrite is proposed. Accept, reject, or adapt each suggestion separately, then reread the surrounding paragraph. Any accepted edit should be applied only after checking the current draft against the quoted source.

## One conceptual precision check

The new reasoning and Diamond Sutra discussion belongs to the essay’s current direction and should not be dismantled. The draft already says that Mark is not constructed as that kind of deliberate Buddhist paradox. Its note on the opening also recognizes that verse 29 qualifies verse 28.

In the final sentence-by-sentence read, keep rhetorical contrast, theological tension, and formal contradiction distinct. A general statement followed by an exception is not, by itself, formally contradictory. The comparison helps us examine fixed descriptions and our way of reading; it does not independently establish a different temporal meaning for Mark’s warning. This is a precision check, not a finding that the present draft commits the error. I would not add another defensive paragraph to address it.

## Rhythm and final reading

Read the tightened paragraphs aloud before changing their individual sentences. Listen especially for repeated “not X, but Y” constructions, abstract nouns with unclear referents, and explanations placed after a sentence that has already landed. Some contrasts are essential to the argument; the aim is variation and clarity, not a ban on their form.

Keep the room around a strong sentence. The only-child passage, the image of a warning entering an already-shaped life, and the final question are places to resist further polishing unless a concrete problem appears. Connective prose should be removed only when the connection remains clear without it.

The time subsection is a good example of the intended restraint. P11–P14 reduce repeated qualifications while preserving Passover, Augustine, John, and Laity and all existing note calls. A more aggressive reduction of witnesses might shorten the section further, but is not recommended here: the traditions are contributing different insights, and the author has deliberately developed those connections.
